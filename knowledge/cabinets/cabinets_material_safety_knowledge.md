# Cabinets — Material Safety Knowledge

## SCORING LANE BOUNDARIES

### What We Score (Health & Chemical Exposure)
- Formaldehyde compliance tier (NAF / ULEF / CARB Phase 2 baseline)
- Adhesive system in substrate (MDI / soy-based / phenol-formaldehyde / urea-formaldehyde)
- Finish VOC content and emissions profile
- Interior surface treatment (sealed vs raw — affects emissions containment)
- Edge banding completeness (sealed = better emissions containment)
- GREENGUARD / GREENGUARD Gold certification status
- KCMA ESP certification status
- Material ingredient disclosure transparency

### What We Do Not Touch
- Construction quality or durability (evaluated separately by Eval Bot)
- Hardware brand or type
- Assembly method or structural integrity
- Aesthetic or style choices
- Kitchen layout or design

**Principle:** "What is this cabinet made of and what does that mean for the people living with it?" We are a materials analyst with data. Not an environmental advocate, not a fear-monger, not an aesthetics judge. Data in, assessment out.

---

## WHY CABINETS ARE THE #1 INDOOR AIR QUALITY CONCERN

A full kitchen's worth of cabinets presents enormous surface area of composite wood continuously off-gassing into the primary living space where people cook and eat. Upper cabinets, lower cabinets, pantry, island — cumulative surface area in a typical kitchen is 200-400+ square feet of composite wood panel. This is the single largest category of composite wood product in most homes, and it sits in the room where you spend the most active time.

The health delta between the best and worst cabinet materials is the largest we've found in any product category evaluated.

## SOURCE MAP

### Tier 1 — Health/Toxicity Primary Sources

**My Chemical-Free House / Corinne Segura (mychemicalfreehouse.net)**
Certified Building Biology Practitioner. Most detailed consumer-facing source on cabinet health/toxicity. Maintains comprehensive list of non-toxic cabinet brands with specific material callouts (NAF plywood, MDI adhesive, PureBond, etc.).

**Key Segura insights we incorporate:**
- Does NOT rely on GREENGUARD Gold to differentiate cabinets — argues most cabinets likely pass GREENGUARD Gold regardless
- Material composition (NAF plywood vs urea-formaldehyde particleboard) is the real differentiator, not certification stickers
- Plywood always held to lower formaldehyde limits than particleboard or MDF, even when both CARB Phase 2 compliant (0.05 ppm vs 0.11 ppm allowable)
- Melamine or laminate finish on surfaces blocks most formaldehyde from off-gassing
- Sealed edges better than exposed edges for emissions containment
- This means IKEA's melamine-coated, edge-sealed particleboard may practically emit less than a "higher quality" cabinet with unfinished interior plywood surfaces

**Main Line Kitchen Design / Paul McAlary**
Publishes detailed blog posts on CARB2 compliance and cabinet off-gassing alongside construction ratings. Useful cross-reference for which brands actually specify NAF or ULEF substrates vs marketing claims.

### Tier 2 — Certification & Regulatory Bodies

**CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI (Federal law as of 2024)**
All composite wood products sold in US must meet formaldehyde emission limits. This is now BASELINE — not a differentiator. Every legal product meets this. What differentiates: ULEF and NAF exceed this minimum significantly.

**GREENGUARD / GREENGUARD Gold (UL Solutions)**
Chamber testing for total VOC and formaldehyde emissions.
- Gold standard: formaldehyde ≤9 μg/m3, total VOCs ≤220 μg/m3
- Tests assembled cabinet, not just substrate material
- **Critical limitation for cabinets:** Per Segura, most cabinets likely pass GREENGUARD Gold regardless, making it a weak differentiator for this specific category. Note status as data point but weight material composition more heavily.

**KCMA ESP (Environmental Stewardship Program)**
Requires 80%+ CARB-compliant pressed wood. Lower bar than GREENGUARD Gold. Participation voluntary. Useful signal but not definitive.

### Tier 3 — Substrate & Material Suppliers

**Columbia Forest Products**: Major supplier of PureBond (soy-based NAF) hardwood plywood. Used by Crystal Cabinets and others specifying NAF. Key brand name in healthy building.

**States Industries**: NAF plywood supplier. Also used by Crystal.

**West Fraser**: Canadian MDF supplier. **Critical detail:** West Pine MDF is NAF (MDI adhesive). Ranger Board MDF has formaldehyde. Same company, two products, completely different health profiles. This is the kind of material-level distinction that matters.

**Arauco / Flakeboard / Roseburg**: Major particleboard and MDF producers. Most are CARB Phase 2 compliant (legal minimum). Some offer ULEF or NAF options.

### Tier 4 — Research & Advocacy

**EWG (Environmental Working Group)** — Formaldehyde health effects data. Useful for contextualizing emission levels.
**EPA Indoor Air Quality** — Baseline formaldehyde guidance and health thresholds.
**California Prop 65** — Warning requirements for formaldehyde-containing products. Useful signal but very broad trigger (most wood products qualify).

## FORMALDEHYDE COMPLIANCE HIERARCHY

| Tier | Standard | Meaning | How to Identify |
|------|----------|---------|-----------------|
| **A+** | NAF (No Added Formaldehyde) | No formaldehyde-based resin used. Adhesive is MDI or soy-based (PureBond). Formaldehyde only from wood's natural content. | Label: "NAF" / "No Added Formaldehyde." Brand examples: Crystal (standard plywood), Columbia Forest Products PureBond |
| **A** | ULEF (Ultra-Low Emitting Formaldehyde) | Uses formaldehyde-based resin but engineered to emit far below CARB Phase 2 limits | Label: "ULEF." Less common in cabinets than in flooring. |
| **B** | CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI | Federal legal minimum since 2024. Limits: HWPW 0.05 ppm, PB 0.09 ppm, MDF 0.11 ppm | Every legal product sold in US meets this. Not a differentiator — it's the floor. |
| **C** | Pre-CARB / Non-compliant | Uses urea-formaldehyde with no emission controls | Imports, unlabeled products, some RTA from non-compliant sources |

**Key insight:** Plywood always held to lower formaldehyde limits than particleboard or MDF under both old and new rules. Even when both are CARB Phase 2 compliant, plywood allowable emissions are roughly half those of MDF (0.05 ppm vs 0.11 ppm). The substrate material IS the story, not the certification sticker.

## ADHESIVE SYSTEMS

| Adhesive | Formaldehyde Content | Common In | Health Profile |
|----------|---------------------|-----------|----------------|
| **MDI (Methylene Diphenyl Diisocyanate)** | None — no formaldehyde | Premium plywood, some MDF (West Pine) | Best. Cures to inert polymer. No ongoing emissions. |
| **Soy-based (PureBond)** | None — no formaldehyde | Columbia Forest Products plywood | Best. Bio-based, no formaldehyde emissions. |
| **Phenol-Formaldehyde (PF)** | Contains formaldehyde but cures more completely | Exterior-grade plywood, some softwood plywood | Good. Higher cure rate means lower ongoing emissions than UF. |
| **Urea-Formaldehyde (UF)** | Primary source of formaldehyde emissions | Most particleboard, most MDF, most interior hardwood plywood | Worst common adhesive. Does not fully cure — continues releasing formaldehyde for years. Accelerated by heat and humidity. |

## EMISSIONS CONTAINMENT — THE SEALING FACTOR

Not all substrates emit equally even with the same adhesive, because surface treatments affect how much formaldehyde escapes into the room.

**Sealed surfaces block emissions:**
- Melamine coating (both sides + edges) — high containment
- UV-cured topcoat on interior surfaces — good containment
- Catalyzed conversion varnish (fully cured) — good containment
- Thermofused laminate — moderate containment

**Unsealed surfaces allow emissions:**
- Raw/unfinished interior box surfaces — no containment
- Exposed edges without edge banding — direct emission pathway
- Air-dried lacquer on interior — minimal containment

**The IKEA Paradox:** IKEA uses particleboard (the substrate we flag as concerning) BUT completely seals it in melamine coating on all surfaces with sealed edges. This creates a barrier that traps formaldehyde inside the substrate. Meanwhile, a "nicer" cabinet using standard plywood but leaving interior surfaces unfinished may emit MORE into the room. Sealed cheap material can outperform unsealed expensive material on actual emissions. We evaluate what's actually reaching the occupant, not just what's in the panel.

## FINISH SYSTEM VOC PROFILES

| Finish Type | VOC Concern | Off-Gassing Duration | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|---------------------|-------|
| **Catalyzed conversion varnish (oven-cured)** | Low after cure | Minimal once fully cured | Most durable AND lowest ongoing emissions. Best-in-class for both eval and material safety. |
| **Catalyzed conversion varnish (air-dried)** | Moderate during cure | Weeks to months | Same chemistry, slower cure = longer off-gassing window. |
| **Lacquer** | Moderate-high | Weeks | Traditional cabinet finish. Higher initial VOCs, dissipates relatively quickly. |
| **Thermofoil (PVC/vinyl)** | Low-moderate | Low ongoing | Main concern is PVC content and phthalicizer plasticizers, not formaldehyde. Delamination failure is eval bot's problem; material composition is ours. |
| **UV-cured coatings** | Very low | Minimal | Instant cure under UV light. Very low residual VOCs. Increasingly used on interior surfaces. |
| **Water-based finishes** | Low | Minimal | Lower durability than catalyzed varnish but better emissions profile. Tradeoff. |

## BRAND HEALTH PROFILES (Key Brands)

**Crystal Cabinets (Princeton, MN)** — Best-in-class production brand for health. NAF plywood standard across ALL lines as of April 2025. Columbia Forest Products PureBond or States Industries NAF plywood. GreenGuard Gold certified finish available. UV-coated interior surfaces on some lines. The benchmark A+ for material safety in cabinets.

**Fabuwood (Newark, NJ)** — All-plywood construction standard. UV-coated interiors (good for emissions containment). Plywood adhesive system not prominently disclosed — needs verification per product. Better than particleboard baseline but NAF status unclear.

**KraftMaid** — Base configuration uses particleboard. Plywood upgrade available (Vantage line and options). Plywood adhesive system needs verification. CARB Phase 2 compliant (baseline legal minimum).

**IKEA SEKTION** — Particleboard substrate with complete melamine sealing on all surfaces and sealed edges. CARB 2 compliant. Health profile is counterintuitively decent due to sealing — the paradox case.

**Puustelli (Finland)** — No MDF, no particleboard, no formaldehyde. Biocomposite components. M1 indoor air classification (Finnish standard, stricter than GREENGUARD Gold). Premium price. Available through Edina MN showroom and dealers.

**Bellmont Cabinet Co. (WA)** — EcoCore option uses pine-core particleboard with no urea formaldehyde. Domestic manufacturer with health-conscious options.

**Merillat / Timberlake / Aristokraft (Builder Grade)** — Typically particleboard or MDF substrate with urea-formaldehyde adhesive. CARB Phase 2 compliant (legal minimum). Likely unfinished or minimally finished interior surfaces. Represents the health floor in branded cabinets.

## SCORING APPROACH

Rate material composition > certification. Certifications are checkboxes; materials are grades.

**Our weighting:**
1. Substrate adhesive system (highest weight — UF vs NAF is the biggest delta)
2. Emissions containment (sealing on all surfaces + edges)
3. Formaldehyde compliance tier
4. Finish system VOC profile
5. Certification status (GREENGUARD, KCMA ESP — noted but not primary)
6. Transparency / disclosure (does brand specify adhesive system and substrate supplier?)

A product with NAF plywood, UV-coated interiors, sealed edges, and catalyzed conversion varnish finish scores highest. A product with UF particleboard, raw interior surfaces, exposed edges, and air-dried lacquer scores lowest. Everything else falls between.
