### Company Background
Lutron Electronics Co. is a privately held company founded in 1961, maintaining independent ownership for over 60 years [SRC-005]. This gives Lutron unusual stability in the smart home market, as they're not subject to the acquisition cycles or profit pressures that affect many competitors. Lutron manufactures across multiple product tiers from entry-level Caseta to professional HomeWorks systems, with consistent engineering standards across all levels. The company's longevity and private ownership structure provides assurance for long-term product support and warranty fulfillment.

### Strengths
- **RF Protocol Reliability**: Clear Connect operates on dedicated frequency band avoiding WiFi network congestion that plagues competing smart switches, delivering "set it and forget it" reliability [SRC-002, SRC-003]
- **LED Compatibility Infrastructure**: Lutron maintains comprehensive LED compatibility database with hundreds of tested bulb models, providing solutions when dimming issues occur [SRC-004]

### Deficiencies
- **Device Count Limitation**: 75-device maximum per Smart Bridge creates hard constraint for larger homes, making whole-house specification inadequate [SRC-001, SRC-002]
- **Basic Aesthetics**: Limited to Diva and Claro switch styles without premium keypad options available in higher Lutron tiers [SRC-001]

### What You Should Know
Caseta represents Lutron's entry point into smart lighting, designed as a stepping stone before upgrading to RadioRA 3 or HomeWorks systems. The system's greatest strength is reliability — Clear Connect RF avoids the connectivity issues that plague WiFi-based smart switches by operating on its own dedicated frequency. This makes Caseta exceptionally stable for everyday use.

However, Caseta's limitations become apparent in larger installations. The 75-device limit and basic app-only scene programming restrict its scalability. The Smart Bridge creates a single point of failure, and switch aesthetics remain basic compared to Lutron's premium lines. Caseta works best for condos, apartments, and smaller homes where device count stays manageable and reliability matters more than advanced features.

### Repair Economics
Smart Bridge failure is the most common issue. Replacement Smart Bridges cost approximately $100, making repair cost-effective versus system replacement. Individual switches rarely fail within the 8-year warranty period.
