Professionals in window treatment dealerships and CEDIA integrators distinguish **quality motorized shades** from novelty gadgets primarily by **integrated motor design** (built-in, precise, quiet operation vs. clamped retrofits), **protocol reliability** (proprietary RF/Zigbee mesh vs. cloud-dependent WiFi), **motor durability** (cycle life >10,000, replaceable components vs. disposable after 2-5 years), and **fabrication precision** (custom cassettes, light gaps <1/8" vs. generic rollers).[1][2][4]

### Professional Hierarchy and Product Rankings
Dealers rank products based on motor quality (e.g., Somfy/Lutron proprietary), integration depth (e.g., Crestron/HomeWorks compatibility), and service call frequency (cloud failures, battery degradation in budget WiFi units).[1][3][4][6] The boundary between "real" motorized shade and "motor clamped to a roller" is **purpose-built tube integration** with headrail cassette, limit calibration, and obstruction sensing vs. retrofit clamps that slip, buzz loudly, and fail after 1-3 years.[4][6]

### Specific Comparisons
- **Lutron Serena vs. Sivoia QS:** Same core Lutron motor (quiet, reliable), but Serena is downmarket (battery-only, retail cassette, no QS wiring/integration).[1]
- **Hunter Douglas Entry PowerView Duette:** Legit pro product (Bluetooth mesh, Somfy-like reliability); entry motors quieter/longer-life than budget but < premium (8k vs. 15k cycles).[4]
- **Springs/Bali/Graber:** Somfy RTS motors; solid retail quality, not premium fab.[1]
- **Budget Blinds:** Resells Hunter Douglas PowerView/Somfy; franchise variability.[4]
- **IKEA:** Not serious; 2-3 yr lifespan, Zigbee flaky without hub.[6]
- **Retrofits (SwitchBot/AXIS):** Not taken seriously; clamps wear tubes (1 yr), noisy (45dB).[6]
- **Tuya WiFi (Yoolax etc.):** Identical OEMs; fails: overheating (1 yr), cloud blackouts, no local control.[5][6]
- **Zigbee (Zemismart):** Slightly better (hub-local) than WiFi but same cheap motors; still Tier 5.[6]

Most warranty calls: Tier 5 WiFi/retrofits (motor jam 40%, cloud 30%).[3][6]
