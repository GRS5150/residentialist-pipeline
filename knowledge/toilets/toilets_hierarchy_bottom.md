Professionals, particularly plumbers and contractors, draw the line between a **good residential toilet** (reliable flush performance ≥600g MaP, durable mechanisms, minimal service calls) and **mediocre/builder-grade** (often <600g MaP, frequent clogs/weak flushes, short lifespan requiring replacement in 5-10 years) primarily using MaP Testing scores as the objective benchmark, supplemented by real-world reliability and parts availability[5][9]. Brands like **American Standard Champion 4 and Gerber Viper** sit on or above the line for value-conscious installs, while **Glacier Bay, Project Source, and most Amazon/DTC brands (e.g., WoodBridge, Swiss Madison, DeerValley)** fall below due to inconsistent MaP performance (<600g common), poor trapway design, and high failure rates despite high Amazon ratings[5].

### MaP Testing Performance Spread (Key Benchmark)
MaP scores measure grams of solid waste flushed in one cycle (soybean paste simulant); ≥600g is **Premium** (highly recommended for residential), 350-600g **acceptable** but clog-prone under heavy use, <350g **not recommended**[5]. Builder-grade (big-box houses) averages 400-600g with gaps; mid-tier varies 600-1000g.

### Plumber/Contractor Consensus on Brands
- **Good Enough for Quality Bathrooms (Tier 3+)**: American Standard (esp. Champion 4: "workhorse" with 4" flush valve, easy repairs), Gerber Viper ("consistent flush, cheap parts"), Mansfield QuantumOne/Denali (reliable pressure-assist, USA-made)[1][8]. These handle 1000g loads, low service calls, 15+ year lifespan.
- **Builder-Grade Filler (Replace in 5-10 Years)**: Glacier Bay/Project Source (weak flushes, cracking china, running valves); DTC like Swiss Madison/WoodBridge/DeerValley (shiny marketing, but thin glazing, poor trapways lead to clogs/stains)[5].
- **Brands Plumbers Refuse/Warn Against**: Glacier Bay/Project Source (most service calls for clogs/valve failures); DTC Amazon brands (e.g., DeerValley: "looks good, flushes like crap after 6 months"). Consensus: Avoid untested <600g MaP for anything but rentals[5].
- **Most Service Calls**: Big-box houses (Glacier Bay et al.) for weak flushes/running; DTC for cracking bowls/difficult trapway access. American Standard/Gerber see fewer post-install[8].

**Floor of Acceptable**: 600g MaP minimum for "good enough" (e.g., basic Cadet/Viper); below risks 2x service calls vs Premium[5].

### Known Issues by Brand
- **American Standard** (post-Lixil 2013 acquisition): No major quality drop; Champion 4/Vormax maintain 1000g MaP, strong parts ecosystem. Some note minor glazing changes, but plumbers rank it top mid-tier[9].
- **Gerber Viper/Avalanche**: Rare cracking; excellent valve reliability (easy DIY flapper swaps). Few running issues[8].
- **Mansfield Denali/Summit**: Solid, but older gravity models <QuantumOne (600g+); occasional seal leaks[1].
- **DTC (Swiss Madison etc.)**: Cracking china (thin walls), weak syphon (double-flush common), hard-to-source parts. High returns despite reviews.
- **Glacier Bay/Project Source**: Frequent running toilets, weak flushes (<500g), china cracks under seat weight.

### DTC Amazon Brands Reality Check
10,000+ review/4.7★ scores reflect easy installs/light use, not pro scrutiny. MaP absence + plumber forums report 30-50% failure in year 1 (clogs, leaks) vs. 10% for American Standard. **Marketing machines, not competent long-term**; plumbers install only for budget flips[5].
