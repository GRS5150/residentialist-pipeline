## Exposure Pathway — Why Countertops Matter

Countertops are food preparation surfaces. Raw meat, produce, bread dough — all contact the surface directly. Children sit at counter height. Spills sit on the surface. Sealers applied to porous stone are reapplied every 1-3 years directly onto the food preparation surface.

The primary exposure pathways are:
- **Direct food contact** with surface material and any sealant/finish
- **VOC off-gassing** from resin binders (engineered quartz, solid surface) into kitchen air
- **Sealer chemistry** applied and reapplied to food preparation surfaces (natural stone, concrete)

## Required Certifications (Context — Not Gate Check)

Unlike faucets (which have mandatory NSF 61/372/UPC gate checks), countertop certifications are voluntary. There is no legal requirement for a countertop to carry Greenguard or NSF 51 before installation. This means certification PRESENCE is a positive signal, but certification ABSENCE alone is not a Red Finding — it's a data point.

**Relevant certifications (in order of Material Safety weight):**
1. **C2C Material Health Certificate** — Third-party-verified ingredient disclosure assessed against 24 health metrics. Strongest available evidence.
2. **ILFI Declare Label** — Ingredient transparency + Red List screening. Red List Free = strong signal.
3. **Greenguard Gold (UL Environment)** — Chamber-tested VOC emissions. ≤220 μg/m³ total VOCs, formaldehyde ≤7.3 ppb. Annual retesting required. Pass/fail only — does NOT disclose exact emission levels.
4. **NSF 51** — Food contact safety certification. Meaningful for countertops because they ARE food contact surfaces.
5. **CARB Phase 2 / EPA TSCA Title VI** — Formaldehyde compliance. Primarily relevant for laminate substrates (particleboard/MDF).
6. **HPD (Health Product Declaration)** — Voluntary composition disclosure. Transparency tool, not a test.
7. **EU equivalents:** French A+ Emissions, German Blue Angel, EU REACH — carry equivalent weight to US certifications per bible Rule 14.

**Greenguard mechanics:**
- Two tiers: Greenguard (≤500 μg/m³) and Greenguard Gold (≤220 μg/m³ + formaldehyde ≤7.3 ppb)
- Tests 360+ individual VOCs. Annual retesting.
- Does NOT test semi-VOCs (phthalates, biocides, flame retardants) or heavy metals.
- Critical limitation: A product at 5 μg/m³ and one at 219 μg/m³ both pass Greenguard Gold.
- Per Rule 14 (Certification Tier Rule): No docking for Greenguard vs Greenguard Gold when both tiers confirm the underlying concern is managed and no credible source has flagged the lower tier as insufficient.

**Countertop brands with Greenguard Gold:** Cambria (+ NSF 51), Silestone (+ NSF), Caesarstone, HanStone (+ NSF 51), Viatera/LG Hausys (+ NSF), Vicostone (+ NSF), Formica laminate, Everform/Formica solid surface, Corian.

## Certification Floor (Rule 15, Cross-Category)

For countertops with relevant certifications and no credible health flags:
- **Certified products with no flags:** 9.5 baseline (Greenguard Gold + NSF 51, or equivalent combination)
- **Sintered stone / porcelain (no resin, no sealer):** 9.8 (eliminates resin off-gassing AND sealer exposure pathways). Reserve headroom above 9.8 for brand-level differentiation within sintered stone class (e.g., Lapitec = silica-free vs Dekton = 5-11% silica).
- **Natural stone (inert) with no sealing needed:** 9.5 (stone itself is safe, dense enough to skip sealer)
- **Natural stone requiring sealing:** 9.5 with Yellow Finding per Rule 13 (Adjacent Product Rule) advising PFAS-free sealer specification

## Active Flags and Concerns

### Flag 1: PFAS in Stone Sealers (Flagged by Habitable + Corinne Segura)
**Status:** ACTIVE CONCERN — credible, documented, actionable.

Most commercial stone sealers use fluoropolymer chemistry containing PFAS. Habitable (formerly Healthy Building Network) flagged this in 2016. EPA study (2009) found high PFAS levels in stone/tile/wood sealers. These sealers are applied directly to FOOD PREPARATION SURFACES and reapplied every 1-3 years. PFAS are persistent, bioaccumulative, linked to hormone disruption, immune effects, and cancer risk.

**Scoring rules (per Rule 13 — Adjacent Product Rule):**
- PFAS concern is about the SEALER (separate product buyer selects), not the stone.
- As long as a comparable PFAS-free sealant is available, the concern goes into Yellow Findings as buyer advisory, NOT into the Material Safety score.
- The score reflects the product being evaluated (the stone), not the companion product (the sealer).

**Confirmed PFAS-free sealers:** TriNova Granite Sealer (confirmed by Green Science Policy Institute, 0-VOC, food-safe), Party Foul Sealer (PFAS-free, water-based), walnut oil/carnauba wax (natural, works on darker stones, 5-7 day cure). Confirmed by Corinne Segura's independent testing.

**Yellow Finding language:** "This stone requires periodic sealing. Most commercial stone sealers contain PFAS (per-and polyfluoroalkyl substances). PFAS-free alternatives exist and are tested — specify PFAS-free sealer at installation and every reapplication."

### Flag 2: Engineered Quartz Resin Off-Gassing (Active Concern in Healthy Homes Community)
**Status:** ACTIVE CONCERN — managed by Greenguard Gold certification.

Engineered quartz contains 6-10% polymer resin binder (polyester or acrylic). Polyester resin contains styrene (under ongoing IARC review). Acrylic resin contains methyl methacrylate. VOC off-gassing from resin is a consumer exposure pathway.

**Scoring rules:**
- Greenguard Gold certification = strong evidence emissions are managed. Score at 9.5 baseline.
- No Greenguard when competitors in same price range carry it = lower score (7-8 range). The absence of certification for a material class with a known active concern is itself a meaningful data point.
- Greenguard (standard, not Gold) = still manages the concern per Rule 14.

### Flag 3: Laminate Substrate Formaldehyde (Managed by CARB Phase 2)
**Status:** MANAGED CONCERN.

Laminate countertops use particleboard or MDF substrates bonded with formaldehyde-based resins. The laminate surface provides containment (IKEA Paradox — sealed substrate outperforms unsealed expensive material on actual occupant exposure).

**Scoring rules:**
- CARB Phase 2 compliant substrate + laminate containment = concern managed. Score normally.
- NAF (no added formaldehyde) substrate = best case within class.
- No CARB compliance information available = lower confidence, flag for verification.

## Not Flags — Do Not Score

### Granite Radon/Radiation — INVESTIGATED, NOT ON RADAR
EPA, Health Canada, AARST, and Health Physics Society have all studied granite radon with large sample sizes (including a million-sample Monte Carlo simulation). Finding: no meaningful contribution to indoor radon levels. The healthy homes community (BBI, Passive House practitioners, ILFI ecosystem) has moved on. NOT a finding. NOT a score impact. Note concern exists in public discourse and state why it does not affect the score.

### Silicosis from Engineered Quartz Cutting — MANUFACTURING HAZARD
219 confirmed US cases of silicosis among quartz countertop fabricators (14 deaths, 26 lung transplants) per CDC/NIOSH data. Australia banned engineered quartz with ≥1% silica content (September 2024). This is a real, serious occupational health crisis — but it is a MANUFACTURING hazard that does not reach the person living with the installed product. Excluded from scoring per Manufacturing vs Consumer Hazard Rule.

### Low-Silica vs High-Silica Quartz — MANUFACTURING DISTINCTION
Cosentino HybriQ+ reduces silica to <40% (or <10% in XM lines). This is a manufacturing safety innovation that reduces fabricator risk. Does NOT affect consumer Material Safety score. Installed quartz is safe regardless of silica content. Not scored differently.

### Antimicrobial Marketing Claims — NO CREDIT
Silestone's Microban and similar antimicrobial treatments receive no positive score benefit. CDC and FDA do not recommend antimicrobial-treated building products for residential settings. Habitable advises against. No evidence of health benefit for the homeowner. Not a penalty either — just no credit.

### Recycled Content — NO AUTOMATIC CREDIT
Recycled content claims receive no automatic positive Material Safety score. Old CRTs (lead-containing glass) have been used as recycled content in some products. If recycled source is transparent and verified = neutral. If unspecified = no benefit.

## Material Class Safety Profiles

### Engineered Quartz
- **Primary concern:** Resin VOC off-gassing (ACTIVE)
- **Managed by:** Greenguard Gold certification
- **Calibrated score:** 9.5 with Greenguard Gold + no other flags (Cambria, Caesarstone, MSI all at 9.5)
- **Lower score triggers:** No Greenguard when competitors carry it, unknown manufacturer with no certifications

### Granite (Dark/Dense — e.g., Ubatuba)
- **Primary concern:** PFAS in sealers (ACTIVE — managed by Rule 13 as Yellow Finding)
- **Stone itself:** Inert, established safe, centuries of use
- **Calibrated score:** 9.5 with Yellow Finding advising PFAS-free sealer
- **Radon:** Investigated, not on radar. Not scored.

### Granite (Light/Porous — e.g., White Ice)
- **Primary concern:** Same as dark granite but higher sealing frequency (every 6-12 months vs 1-2 years) = higher cumulative sealer exposure
- **Calibrated score:** 9.5 with Yellow Finding (same as dark granite — frequency difference noted in advisory, not in score)

### Quartzite (True Quartzite)
- **Profile:** Natural stone, established safe. Sealant = PFAS concern per above.
- **Watch:** Mislabeled Stone Rule — many "quartzites" are actually dolomitic marble. Material Safety profile doesn't change (both are safe stone) but durability expectations are set wrong.

### Marble
- **Profile:** Natural stone, established safe. Higher sealing frequency than granite = higher cumulative PFAS exposure potential. Same scoring treatment as granite with Yellow Finding.

### Sintered Stone / Porcelain (e.g., Dekton)
- **Profile:** No resin, no binder, no sealer needed. Manufactured at extreme temperatures that burn off organic compounds. Mineral composition similar to fired porcelain — established safe material class.
- **Calibrated score:** 9.8 (Dekton Aura 15). Eliminates both resin and sealer exposure pathways.
- **Headroom:** 9.8 rather than 10 to allow brand-level differentiation (Lapitec = first silica-free sintered stone vs Dekton = 5-11% silica content).

### Solid Surface — Acrylic (e.g., Corian)
- **Profile:** Acrylic resin (PMMA) low-risk once cured. No sealing needed = no PFAS. Major brands carry Greenguard Gold.
- **Watch:** Polyester resin blends contain styrene — same concern as engineered quartz resin but at lower percentages. Pure acrylic > polyester blends on Material Safety.

### Laminate
- **Profile:** Substrate formaldehyde managed by CARB Phase 2 + laminate containment. Melamine overlay is inert once cured.
- **Key question:** CARB Phase 2 compliance of the substrate.

### Butcher Block / Wood
- **Profile:** Solid wood is established safe (centuries of use, no healthy homes community concerns). Adhesive system and finish/sealant are the exposure pathways.
- **Key question:** FDA food-contact compliance of the finish. Food-safe oil finishes (mineral oil, walnut oil) = no concerns. Film finishes (polyurethane) may off-gas during cure, inert once cured.

### Concrete
- **Profile:** Concrete is established safe (calcium-based, centuries of use). The SEALER is the consumer exposure pathway — parallels natural stone. Score the sealer, not the concrete.

### Stainless Steel
- **Profile:** Inert. No exposure pathway concerns. Highest material safety confidence in countertop category alongside sintered stone.
