## 1. CATEGORY STRUCTURE — HOW COUNTERTOPS DIFFER FROM OTHER CATEGORIES

Countertops do NOT evaluate like cabinets or faucets. In those categories, the evaluation unit is a branded product from a manufacturer (IKEA SEKTION, Moen Align). Countertops split into two fundamentally different structures:

**Branded engineered products** — Caesarstone, Silestone, Cambria, Corian, Dekton. These have a manufacturer, a product line, consistent material composition, and spec sheets. They evaluate more like cabinets.

**Commodity natural materials** — Granite, marble, quartzite, soapstone. There is no "manufacturer." There are quarries, distributors, and fabricators. Every slab varies. Quality of the installed product depends heavily on the fabricator.

**Consequence:** Countertops use a two-layer evaluation:
- **Layer 1: Material Class Profile** — not scored, not ranked against other classes. Documents the trade-off landscape honestly.
- **Layer 2: Within-Class Product Evaluation** — scored. Compares products within the same material class against each other.

The report for a buyer is NOT "quartz is better than granite." It IS "here's what each material means for your life, and within the material your builder is speccing, here's how this specific product rates."

## 2. MATERIAL CLASS TAXONOMY

### 2.1 Engineered Quartz
**What it is:** Factory-manufactured slabs. Ground natural quartz (90-94%) bound with polymer resin (6-10%, typically polyester or acrylic). Pigments added for color/pattern. Manufactured under high pressure.
**Key brands:** Caesarstone, Silestone (Cosentino), Cambria, MSI Q Premium, Viatera (LG Hausys), HanStone, Polarstone, Allen + Roth (Lowe's house brand)
**Manufacturing standard:** Breton S.p.A. (Italy) developed the original compaction process. Products manufactured on Breton equipment or equivalent high-pressure systems represent the industry standard. Non-Breton manufacturing processes exist and may produce inconsistent slab density and resin distribution.

**What it does well:**
- Near-zero maintenance — no sealing required
- Highly consistent color and pattern (manufactured, not natural variation)
- Stain resistant (non-porous when properly manufactured)
- Wide color/pattern range including marble-look options
- NSF 51 certified for food contact (most major brands)

**Achilles heel:**
- **Heat sensitivity.** Resin binder can scorch, discolor, or crack under direct heat. Hot pans directly on the surface can cause permanent damage. This is the #1 real-world complaint.
- **UV sensitivity.** Prolonged direct sunlight can cause yellowing or color shift in some colors, particularly whites. Not suitable for outdoor applications.
- **Not repairable for major damage.** Deep chips or cracks typically require slab replacement, not repair.

**Maintenance reality:** Essentially maintenance-free. No sealing. Clean with mild soap and water. Avoid harsh chemicals (bleach can damage resin over time). Avoid direct heat — always use trivets.

**Repairability:** Minor chips can sometimes be filled with color-matched epoxy by a professional. Major damage = replacement section or full replacement. Cannot be sanded/refinished like solid surface.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: LOW — resin binder vulnerable. Maximum ~150°F sustained contact before risk of damage.
- Stain: HIGH — non-porous surface resists most household stains when cleaned promptly.
- Scratch: MODERATE — harder than solid surface, softer than granite/quartzite. Can scratch; scratches are not repairable.
- Impact: MODERATE — can chip at edges and corners, especially thin profiles.

**Installation sensitivity:** MODERATE. Requires experienced fabricator for templating, cutting, and seam placement. Seam quality varies significantly by fabricator. Support requirements for overhangs are critical (unsupported overhangs crack).

**Material Safety profile:** ACTIVE CONCERN in healthy homes community. Resin binder contains styrene (polyester resin) or methyl methacrylate (acrylic resin). Styrene is under ongoing IARC review. VOC off-gassing from resin is a consumer exposure pathway — score conservatively per Healthy Homes Radar framework. Silicosis from cutting is a MANUFACTURING hazard — excluded from scoring per Manufacturing vs Consumer Hazard Rule.

**Typical installed price range:** $55–$120/sqft (varies by brand, color, edge profile, market)

**Within-class quality differentiators:**
- [ ] Breton vs non-Breton manufacturing (TO CONFIRM with sources)
- [ ] Resin percentage and type (polyester vs acrylic)
- [ ] Slab thickness options (2cm vs 3cm)
- [ ] Brand warranty terms and transferability
- [ ] Manufacturing location transparency
- [ ] NSF 51 food contact certification
- [ ] Greenguard/Greenguard Gold certification (some brands carry this)
- [ ] Opacity of manufacturing process for no-name/import brands

**Red flags within class:**
- No identifiable manufacturing facility
- No Breton or equivalent process documentation
- Manufacturing process opaque — cannot verify resin type or quartz content
- No NSF 51 certification
- No Greenguard certification when competitors in same price range carry it
- Warranty excludes heat damage without prominent buyer disclosure

### 2.2 Natural Stone — Granite
**What it is:** Igneous rock formed from cooled magma. Composed primarily of quartz, feldspar, and mica. Quarried worldwide. Every slab is unique.

**What it does well:**
- **Heat resistant.** Can handle hot pans directly — one of the few countertop materials where this is genuinely safe.
- Extremely hard (6–6.5 Mohs scale)
- Scratch resistant
- Unique natural appearance — no two slabs identical
- Long track record — decades of residential use with known performance

**Achilles heel:**
- **Requires periodic sealing.** Granite is porous. Unsealed or poorly sealed granite stains. Sealing frequency depends on stone density — some granites need annual sealing, dense varieties may go 3-5 years.
- Seams are visible and permanent
- Heavy — requires robust cabinet support
- Limited color consistency (natural variation — a feature for some, a problem for others)

**Maintenance reality:** Seal every 1-3 years depending on stone density and porosity. Clean with stone-specific cleaner or mild soap. Avoid acidic cleaners. Water test to check seal integrity (water droplets should bead, not absorb).

**Repairability:** Chips can be filled with color-matched epoxy. Cracks are more difficult — may require professional repair or section replacement. Cannot be refinished to "like new" without professional polishing.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: HIGH — natural stone handles heat well. One of the strongest performers here.
- Stain: MODERATE (when sealed) to LOW (unsealed). Porous stone absorbs oils, wine, acidic liquids.
- Scratch: HIGH — very hard surface. Daily kitchen use will not scratch it.
- Impact: MODERATE — can chip at edges, especially with thin profiles or bullnose edges.

**Installation sensitivity:** MODERATE to HIGH. Fabricator quality matters for seam placement, edge finishing, sink cutout precision, and initial sealing application. Template accuracy is critical.

**Material Safety profile:** Granite radon/radiation is INVESTIGATED — NOT CURRENTLY ON RADAR per EPA, Health Canada, AARST, Health Physics Society (million-sample Monte Carlo simulation). Not a finding. Not scored. See Healthy Homes Radar framework, Section 3 of bible.

**Typical installed price range:** $40–$100/sqft (Level 1/builder grade at low end, exotic/premium slabs at high end)

**Within-class quality differentiators:**
- Stone density and porosity (affects sealing frequency and stain resistance)
- Slab thickness (2cm vs 3cm — 3cm standard for kitchen, 2cm acceptable for bath)
- Slab grading level (Level 1-5 at most distributors — reflects rarity and aesthetics, not necessarily durability)
- Fabricator quality (edge work, seam tightness, initial seal application)
- Country of origin (affects stone characteristics but is not a quality ranking — Brazilian, Indian, Italian granites all vary within origin)

**Red flags within class:**
- 2cm slab spec'd for kitchen island without laminated edge (structural concern)
- No sealing included in installation or no sealing guidance provided to buyer
- "Lifetime" claims without maintenance disclosure
- Builder spec says "granite" without identifying stone type or distributor

### 2.3 Natural Stone — Quartzite
**What it is:** Metamorphic rock formed when sandstone is subjected to extreme heat and pressure. Composed of 90-99% quartz crystals. Naturally occurring — not to be confused with engineered quartz (manufactured product). Hardness ~7 on Mohs scale.

**What it does well:**
- **Hardest common countertop material.** Outperforms granite on scratch resistance.
- Heat resistant (natural stone, no resin to damage)
- Etch resistant — does not react to acids like marble does
- Aesthetic appeal comparable to marble but with dramatically better performance
- Does not require the level of babying that marble does

**Achilles heel:**
- **Price.** Premium quartzite is among the most expensive countertop options.
- Still requires sealing (though less porous than many granites)
- Seam visibility — like all natural stone
- Fabrication is difficult and expensive due to hardness — not all fabricators are equipped
- Some quartzites have natural fissures that can be mistaken for cracks

**Maintenance reality:** Seal every 1-2 years. Less porous than granite on average but still requires maintenance. Some dense quartzites approach near-zero-porosity and need very infrequent sealing.

**Repairability:** Similar to granite. Chips can be filled. Major cracks are difficult. Professional polishing possible but expensive due to stone hardness.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: HIGH — natural stone, handles heat well.
- Stain: HIGH (when sealed) — less porous than most granite.
- Scratch: VERY HIGH — hardest common countertop material.
- Impact: MODERATE — can chip at edges despite hardness (hardness ≠ toughness).

**Installation sensitivity:** HIGH. Quartzite's hardness makes it difficult to fabricate. Requires experienced fabricator with proper equipment. Poor fabrication can cause chipping at cutouts and edges. Support requirements for overhangs similar to granite.

**Material Safety profile:** Natural stone with centuries of residential use. Not on any healthy homes radar. Sealant chemistry is the only consumer exposure pathway — score the sealant, not the stone.

**Typical installed price range:** $70–$200+/sqft (wide range depending on variety and rarity)

**CRITICAL: Mislabeled Stone Rule (see Section 4)**
The term "soft quartzite" does not exist as a geological category. Any stone marketed as "soft quartzite" is marble or dolomitic marble that has been mislabeled. The system must reclassify these stones under the Marble material class. See Section 4 for the full rule and known mislabeled stones list.

### 2.4 Natural Stone — Marble
**What it is:** Metamorphic rock formed from limestone under heat and pressure. Composed primarily of calcite. Softness range 3-5 on Mohs scale. Quarried worldwide — Italy (Carrara, Calacatta), India, Greece, Turkey, Spain among major sources.

**What it does well:**
- **Aesthetics.** Marble's veining and luminosity are genuinely unmatched. There's a reason it's been prized for millennia.
- Cool surface temperature (valued in baking applications)
- Can be polished to a high gloss
- Some varieties (dolomitic/hard marble) offer improved durability over classic calcite marble

**Achilles heel:**
- **Etches from acid contact.** Lemon juice, vinegar, wine, tomato sauce — all leave dull marks on polished marble. This is chemical, not stain-related, and happens even on sealed marble.
- **Stains relatively easily.** Porous and absorbs liquids.
- **Scratches.** Soft stone. Daily kitchen use will show wear over time.
- **Chips.** Edges and corners are vulnerable.

**Application flag — Marble in kitchens:** Marble is functionally compromised for kitchen countertop use due to acid etching from common kitchen substances. This is not a defect — it's a material characteristic. Reports must flag this clearly: "Marble in a kitchen requires a buyer who understands and accepts that the surface will develop a patina over time from normal kitchen use. If the buyer expects a pristine surface, marble is not the right material for a kitchen application. Marble is excellent for bathroom vanities, fireplace surrounds, and decorative applications where acid contact is rare."

**Maintenance reality:** Seal 2-4 times per year for kitchen use. Even sealed, marble will etch from acids (sealer protects against stains, not etching — this distinction is critical and most buyers don't understand it). Honed finish shows etching less than polished.

**Repairability:** Professional polishing can remove light etching and scratches. Chips can be filled. Marble is actually more repairable than engineered quartz in this sense — a skilled stone restoration professional can make marble look new again. The catch is it will need it more often.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: MODERATE to HIGH — natural stone handles heat, but thermal shock can crack thin slabs.
- Stain: LOW — porous, absorbs liquids readily.
- Scratch: LOW — soft stone, scratches from daily use.
- Impact: LOW — chips easily at edges.
- **Etch resistance: VERY LOW** — this is the defining vulnerability.

**Installation sensitivity:** MODERATE. Standard natural stone fabrication. Seam placement matters more than with harder stones because seams in marble can be more visible. Edge profiles should avoid thin, delicate options (they chip).

**Material Safety profile:** Natural stone with millennia of residential use. Not on any healthy homes radar. Sealant chemistry is the only consumer exposure pathway — score the sealant, not the stone.

**Typical installed price range:** $50–$180+/sqft (Carrara at the accessible end, Calacatta Gold at the premium end)

**Within-class quality differentiators:**
- Marble type (calcite marble vs dolomitic/hard marble — significant durability difference)
- Stone density and porosity
- Veining consistency and structural soundness (heavy veining can indicate structural weakness)
- Slab thickness
- Finish type (polished vs honed vs leathered — honed/leathered hide wear better)
- Fabricator quality

**Red flags within class:**
- Builder speccing marble kitchen counters without explicit buyer disclosure about etching
- "Soft quartzite" label on what is actually marble (see Mislabeled Stone Rule, Section 4)
- Polished finish specified for heavy-use kitchen (honed would be more practical)
- No sealing protocol provided to buyer

### 2.5 Solid Surface (Acrylic/Polyester)
**What it is:** Factory-manufactured sheets/slabs. Blend of acrylic or polyester resin with mineral fillers (aluminum trihydrate — derived from bauxite). Major innovation by DuPont (Corian, 1967). Non-porous, solid-through color.
**Key brands:** Corian (DuPont/CorianDesign), Staron (Samsung), Hi-Macs (LG Hausys), Formica Solid Surface, Wilsonart Solid Surface, Meganite, Avonite

**What it does well:**
- **Seamless installation.** Seams can be chemically bonded and sanded invisible — unique among countertop materials.
- **Fully repairable.** Scratches, burns, and minor damage can be sanded out. The color goes all the way through.
- Non-porous — no sealing required
- Integrated sink bowls (same material, no seam at sink = hygienic, no leak point)
- Thermoformable — can be shaped for curved applications
- Warm to the touch compared to stone

**Achilles heel:**
- **Scratches easily.** Softer than any stone or quartz product. Daily kitchen use will show scratches.
- **Heat damage.** Hot pans will scorch or melt the surface.
- **Looks manufactured.** Despite improvements, solid surface doesn't fully replicate natural stone aesthetics. This is perception, not performance — but it affects resale and buyer satisfaction.

**Maintenance reality:** No sealing. Clean with mild soap. Scratches can be buffed out with Scotch-Brite pad or fine sandpaper (this is a genuine advantage — homeowner can maintain it). Deeper damage requires professional sanding.

**Repairability:** EXCELLENT — the best in the category. Color runs through the full thickness. Scratches, burns, small chips can all be sanded and polished out. Major damage can be cut out and a new section seamlessly bonded in. This is the only countertop material where the homeowner can realistically repair damage themselves.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: LOW — will scorch/melt. Always use trivets.
- Stain: HIGH — non-porous, most stains wipe off or can be sanded out.
- Scratch: LOW — soft material, scratches readily. BUT scratches are repairable.
- Impact: MODERATE — more flexible than stone, less prone to chipping, but can crack under severe impact.

**Installation sensitivity:** MODERATE. Requires certified fabricator for seamless bonding. Quality of seam work is the primary differentiator between good and bad installations.

**Material Safety profile:** Acrylic resin (PMMA) is generally considered low-risk for consumer exposure once cured. Polyester resin formulations may contain styrene — same concern as engineered quartz resin but at lower concentages. Higher acrylic content = generally better material safety profile. Corian is 1/3 acrylic resin, 2/3 mineral filler. Score based on resin type and content.

**Typical installed price range:** $40–$85/sqft

**Within-class quality differentiators:**
- Acrylic content percentage (higher = more durable, more repairable, more expensive)
- Pure acrylic vs polyester blend (pure acrylic is premium — Corian, Hi-Macs. Polyester blends are budget.)
- Color-through consistency (some budget products have color concentration at the surface)
- Warranty terms
- Certified fabricator network (brand-backed installation vs generic)

**Red flags within class:**
- Polyester-based product marketed as equivalent to acrylic-based (different durability and repairability)
- No certified fabricator program (seam quality is everything in this material)
- Warranty that excludes heat damage without buyer disclosure

### 2.6 Sintered Stone / Porcelain Slab
**What it is:** Ultra-compact surface manufactured by pressing natural raw materials (minerals, porcelain, glass) under extreme heat (~1200°C) and pressure. Essentially a very large-format porcelain tile. Relatively new in the residential countertop market.
**Key brands:** Dekton (Cosentino), Neolith, Lapitec, Laminam, SapienStone

**What it does well:**
- **Highest heat resistance of any countertop material.** Can handle direct flame — used as cooktop surfaces in some applications.
- UV resistant — suitable for outdoor kitchens (unique advantage)
- Extremely scratch resistant
- Stain resistant — near-zero porosity
- No sealing required
- Very thin profile options available (8mm, 12mm) for modern aesthetics

**Achilles heel:**
- **Can crack or chip under point impact.** Despite hardness, sintered stone is brittle. Dropping a heavy object on it can cause damage that's difficult or impossible to repair.
- **Not repairable.** Damage typically requires section or full replacement.
- **Relatively new.** Long-term residential track record is shorter than stone or quartz. 10-15 years of widespread residential use vs 30+ for quartz, centuries for stone.
- **Fabrication requires specialized equipment.** Not all fabricators are equipped or trained.

**Maintenance reality:** Essentially zero maintenance. No sealing, no special cleaners. The most maintenance-free option available.

**Repairability:** POOR. The material's extreme hardness means damage is difficult to repair. Chips can sometimes be filled but repairs are visible. Cracks typically require replacement.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: VERY HIGH — best in category. Can handle direct heat that would destroy quartz or solid surface.
- Stain: VERY HIGH — near-zero porosity.
- Scratch: VERY HIGH — extremely hard surface.
- Impact: LOW to MODERATE — brittle. This is the trade-off for all that hardness.

**Installation sensitivity:** HIGH. Requires fabricator specifically trained and equipped for sintered/porcelain slab. Material is unforgiving — fabrication errors mean slab replacement, not repair. Fewer qualified fabricators available than for stone or quartz.

**Material Safety profile:** Manufactured at temperatures that burn off organic compounds. No resin binder (unlike engineered quartz). Mineral composition similar to fired porcelain — established safe material class. Not on any healthy homes radar. One of the cleanest material safety profiles in the countertop category.

**Typical installed price range:** $65–$150/sqft

**Within-class quality differentiators:**
- [ ] Brand-specific manufacturing process and quality control
- [ ] Slab thickness options and appropriate application guidance
- [ ] Warranty terms (especially impact/chip coverage)
- [ ] Fabricator network and training requirements
- [ ] Track record length

### 2.7 Laminate
**What it is:** Layers of kraft paper impregnated with resin, topped with a decorative layer and protective melamine overlay, bonded to a particleboard or MDF substrate. High-pressure laminate (HPL) is significantly more durable than low-pressure/direct-pressure laminate.
**Key brands:** Formica, Wilsonart, Pionite, Arborite

**What it does well:**
- **Most affordable countertop option by a significant margin.**
- Huge color/pattern selection
- Low installation sensitivity — most countertop installers can handle it
- Modern HPL products are significantly better than the laminate of 20 years ago
- Lightweight — no special cabinet reinforcement needed
- No sealing required

**Achilles heel:**
- **Cannot be repaired.** Chips, burns, deep scratches, and delamination are permanent.
- **Edge vulnerability.** Seams and edges are the failure point — water infiltration causes substrate swelling.
- **Heat damage.** Hot pans will scorch or blister the surface permanently.
- **Perception.** Laminate carries a "budget" stigma that affects resale regardless of actual quality.
- **Shorter lifespan than any stone/quartz/solid surface option.** 10-20 years vs 20-lifetime for other materials.

**Maintenance reality:** Minimal daily maintenance. But damage is irreversible — there is no refinishing, no polishing, no sanding out scratches. Prevention is the only maintenance strategy.

**Repairability:** POOR. Minor chips can sometimes be addressed with color-matched repair paste, but results are visible. Burn marks, delamination, and edge swelling cannot be fixed — only replaced.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: LOW — burns/blisters from hot pans.
- Stain: MODERATE to HIGH — surface is non-porous, but certain stains can penetrate if left.
- Scratch: LOW to MODERATE — better than solid surface, worse than any stone.
- Impact: LOW — chips at edges, dents from heavy impact.

**Installation sensitivity:** LOW. Most straightforward countertop installation.

**Material Safety profile:** Substrate is particleboard or MDF — formaldehyde binder concerns apply, but the laminate surface provides containment (IKEA Paradox parallel). CARB Phase 2 compliance of the substrate matters. The melamine overlay itself is inert once cured. Follow the Material Safety Investigation Sequence — check for Greenguard/CARB compliance on the specific product.

**Typical installed price range:** $15–$45/sqft

**Tier expectation flag:** Laminate specified in a home at $250+/sqft construction cost is unusual for the tier and worth flagging as a value finding (not a quality finding — laminate isn't bad, it's just unexpected at that price point).

**Within-class quality differentiators:**
- HPL vs low-pressure laminate (significant durability difference)
- Substrate material (particleboard vs MDF vs moisture-resistant particleboard)
- Edge treatment (post-formed/rolled edge vs separate edge strip — post-formed is more durable)
- Thickness of laminate layer
- Brand warranty
- CARB Phase 2 compliance of substrate

### 2.8 Butcher Block / Wood
**What it is:** Solid wood surface, typically constructed from strips of hardwood glued together. Available in end-grain (checkerboard pattern, self-healing, premium), edge-grain (long strips showing edge, most common), and face-grain (long strips showing face, decorative, least durable for countertop use).
**Common species:** Hard maple (most popular for kitchen), walnut, cherry, white oak, teak, beech

**What it does well:**
- **Warmth and character.** The only countertop material that gets better looking with age when properly maintained.
- **Repairable.** Can be sanded down and refinished multiple times over its lifetime.
- Naturally antimicrobial (hardwood — maple in particular)
- Softer impact surface — dropped dishes less likely to break
- Can be cut on directly (end-grain) — functions as both countertop and cutting surface

**Achilles heel:**
- **Moisture and dimensional movement.** Wood expands and contracts with humidity changes — up to 1/8" movement is normal and must be accounted for in installation. Installer MUST understand wood movement or joints will fail.
- **Water damage.** Standing water, especially at sink cutouts, will damage wood. Proper sealing around sinks is critical.
- **Maintenance intensity.** Requires regular oiling or sealing to maintain protection.
- **Bacteria concerns.** Despite natural antimicrobial properties of hardwood, health departments and inspectors sometimes flag wood countertops — buyer should be aware of local code implications.

**Maintenance reality:** Oil-finished surfaces need re-oiling every 1-6 months depending on use. Film-finish (polyurethane/conversion varnish) needs less frequent maintenance but cannot be used as a cutting surface and is harder to repair. Waterlox, Rubio Monocoat, and mineral oil are common treatment options — each with different maintenance cycles and performance characteristics.

**Repairability:** EXCELLENT — second only to solid surface and in some ways better (wood ages gracefully, solid surface just returns to factory appearance). Can be sanded and refinished. Burns, stains, and scratches can be sanded out. Deep gouges can be filled. The surface can be completely renewed multiple times over its lifespan.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: LOW to MODERATE — hot pans will burn/scorch the finish and potentially the wood. Always use trivets.
- Stain: MODERATE (when properly sealed/oiled) to LOW (neglected). Red wine, beets, and berries are common staining agents.
- Scratch: LOW — wood scratches from kitchen use. But scratches add character in this material and are repairable.
- Impact: MODERATE — softer surface absorbs impact rather than chipping.

**Installation sensitivity:** HIGH. The installer must understand wood dimensional movement. Fastening methods must allow for expansion/contraction. Sink cutout waterproofing is critical. Improper installation is the #1 cause of butcher block failure. This is one of the few countertop materials where the installer matters as much as the product.

**Material Safety profile:** Solid wood is one of the safest material classes — established safe material per Healthy Homes Radar (centuries of use, no concerns from healthy homes community). The adhesive system (if any — some butcher blocks are glued) and the finish/sealant are the exposure pathways. Food-safe oil finishes (mineral oil, walnut oil) have no concerns. Film finishes (polyurethane) may have VOC off-gassing during cure but are inert once cured. FDA food-contact compliance of the finish is the key question.

**Typical installed price range:** $40–$120/sqft (pine/commodity hardwood at low end, end-grain walnut at high end)

**Within-class quality differentiators:**
- **Construction type:** End-grain >> Edge-grain >> Face-grain for durability and self-healing properties
- **Wood species:** Hard maple is the standard for kitchen. Walnut, cherry, white oak are premium alternatives. Pine/poplar/bamboo are budget options with significantly lower durability.
- **Adhesive system:** What's holding the strips together? FDA food-safe adhesive? Formaldehyde-based?
- **Thickness:** 1.5" is standard. Thinner is less durable and more prone to warping.
- **Finish type and quality:** Oil vs film vs hybrid. Each has trade-offs.
- **Grain direction consistency and joint quality**
- **Kiln-dried vs air-dried lumber** (affects dimensional stability)

**Red flags within class:**
- Face-grain construction marketed for kitchen countertop use (it's decorative, not functional)
- Softwood species (pine, poplar) for kitchen application without buyer disclosure
- No guidance provided on maintenance schedule or finish type
- Installer does not account for dimensional movement (rigid fastening to cabinets = future failure)
- Unknown adhesive system in budget imported products

### 2.9 Concrete
**What it is:** Cast concrete countertops — either precast in a shop and installed, or cast in place. Typically a custom/semi-custom product made by specialized fabricators. Concrete is mixed with reinforcement (fiberglass, rebar, wire mesh) and finished with sealers. Integral pigments provide color.

**What it does well:**
- **Fully customizable.** Shape, color, embedded objects, integrated sinks/drain boards — nearly unlimited design flexibility.
- **Unique aesthetic.** Industrial, organic, or artisan appearance not achievable with manufactured products.
- Extremely heavy and solid-feeling
- Can be made locally (supports local artisan fabricators)

**Achilles heel:**
- **Cracking.** Concrete cracks. Hairline cracks are considered normal and expected — this must be communicated to the buyer. The question is whether the buyer considers this patina or a defect.
- **Sealing intensity.** Concrete is porous and requires regular sealing. Sealer failure leads to staining.
- **Weight.** Very heavy — requires robust cabinet support, possibly structural reinforcement.
- **Highly fabricator-dependent.** Quality varies enormously based on the skill of the concrete artisan. This is the most fabricator-dependent countertop material.
- **Curing and sealing chemistry.** The sealer is the functional surface the buyer lives with. Sealer quality and reapplication discipline determine the experience.

**Maintenance reality:** Wax every 1-3 months for wax-sealed surfaces. Penetrating sealers need reapplication every 1-3 years. Some high-performance sealers (epoxy-based) provide longer protection but change the surface feel. Stains from acidic substances if sealer is compromised.

**Repairability:** MODERATE. Hairline cracks can be filled. Surface can be resealed and honed. Major structural cracks are difficult to address. Color inconsistency between original pour and repair patches is common.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: HIGH — concrete handles heat well (with appropriate sealer).
- Stain: LOW to MODERATE — highly dependent on sealer quality and maintenance.
- Scratch: MODERATE — concrete surface scratches but can be resurfaced.
- Impact: HIGH for the concrete itself, but edges/corners can chip.

**Installation sensitivity:** VERY HIGH. This is effectively a custom-fabricated product. Fabricator skill is the single biggest quality variable. Cast-in-place requires experienced concrete countertop specialist. Precast requires precise templating and careful transport/installation (concrete is heavy and brittle during handling).

**Material Safety profile:** Concrete itself is an established safe material — calcium-based, centuries of use, not on any healthy homes radar. The SEALER is the consumer exposure pathway. Concrete countertop sealers range from food-safe mineral/wax sealers to high-VOC epoxy systems. Score the sealer, not the concrete. This parallels the sealant-scoring approach for natural stone.

**Typical installed price range:** $70–$150+/sqft (highly variable — custom fabrication costs drive pricing)

### 2.10 Stainless Steel
**What it is:** Sheet stainless steel (typically Type 304 or 316) formed over a substrate (plywood, MDF, or particleboard core) or solid gauge steel with integrated features. Common in commercial kitchens, increasingly used in residential.

**What it does well:**
- **Hygienic.** Non-porous, easy to sanitize. There's a reason commercial kitchens use it.
- Heat resistant — can handle hot pans directly
- Stain resistant — truly non-porous
- No sealing required
- Impervious to water damage (when properly fabricated)
- Integrated sink options (welded, seamless = maximally hygienic)

**Achilles heel:**
- **Scratches.** Stainless steel scratches from normal use. Over time, it develops a brushed patina. This is either character or damage depending on the buyer.
- **Noise.** Metal surfaces are louder than stone or solid surface.
- **Fingerprints and water spots** (aesthetic, not functional, but a daily nuisance for some)
- **Dents.** Thin-gauge steel dents from impact.

**Maintenance reality:** Minimal. Wipe with stainless cleaner or soap and water. Scratches are unavoidable but develop into an even patina over time.

**Repairability:** Scratches cannot be removed — they accumulate into a patina. Dents in thin-gauge steel are difficult to fix. Well-made stainless countertops rarely need repair beyond cosmetic concerns.

**Heat / stain / scratch / impact resistance:**
- Heat: HIGH — handles direct heat.
- Stain: VERY HIGH — non-porous metal.
- Scratch: LOW — scratches readily (but develops acceptable patina).
- Impact: DEPENDENT ON GAUGE. 14-16 gauge = good impact resistance. Thinner = dent-prone.

**Installation sensitivity:** MODERATE. Requires metal fabrication shop — not a standard countertop installer.

**Material Safety profile:** Stainless steel is inert. No exposure pathway concerns. Highest material safety confidence in the countertop category along with fired porcelain/sintered stone and natural stone.

**Typical installed price range:** $80–$200/sqft (highly variable based on gauge, fabrication complexity)

**Critical within-class differentiator: GAUGE.**
- 14 gauge (1.9mm) — Commercial grade. Excellent durability. Premium residential.
- 16 gauge (1.5mm) — Standard quality residential. Good balance of cost and durability.
- 18 gauge (1.2mm) — Acceptable for residential with care. Dent-prone under impact.
- 20+ gauge (1.0mm or thinner) — **Significant quality concern.** Dents easily, feels flimsy, inadequate for kitchen countertop application. Score hit should be substantial.

**Red flags within class:**
- Gauge not disclosed (if you have to ask, it's probably thin)
- Gauge thinner than 18 for kitchen countertop application
- No sound-dampening undercoating (noise becomes a livability issue)
- Substrate quality unknown (plywood vs particleboard matters for moisture near sinks)

## 3. COUNTERTOP-SPECIFIC EVALUATION RULES

### Rule: No Cross-Class Ranking
Material classes are NOT ranked against each other. The system does not say "quartzite is better than granite" or "quartz is better than solid surface." Each class has trade-offs that depend on the buyer's priorities, usage patterns, and maintenance tolerance. The report presents Material Class Profiles honestly and lets the buyer decide which trade-off set works for their life.

**Exceptions — contextual flags the system DOES make:**
- Laminate specified at $250+/sqft construction cost → flag as unusual for tier (value finding, not quality finding)
- Marble specified for kitchen primary work surface → flag application mismatch and disclose etching reality
- Material class of what's delivered doesn't match what was spec'd or promised → Red Finding (misrepresentation)

### Rule: Within-Class Scoring Only
Quality scores compare products within the same material class. Caesarstone vs Silestone vs MSI Q. Corian vs Staron vs Hi-Macs. Not Caesarstone vs Corian. The buyer sees how their specific product rates against its peers.

### Rule: Fabricator Matters (Natural Stone and Concrete)
For natural stone and concrete, the fabricator is a quality variable that doesn't exist in manufactured products. The system acknowledges this in the report: "The quality of a granite or concrete countertop depends significantly on the fabricator's skill. Edge work, seam placement, cutout precision, and initial sealing application all vary by fabricator. This evaluation scores the material and product selection — fabricator quality is a separate variable the buyer should verify independently."

The system does NOT attempt to score individual fabricators. That's a future consideration if the database grows to include fabricator reviews, but at launch it's out of scope.

### Rule: Sealer Is a Separate Evaluation Layer
For natural stone, concrete, and (to a lesser degree) butcher block, the sealer is a critical component that determines the buyer's day-to-day experience. The system treats sealer quality as part of the Material Class Profile's maintenance assessment. Specific sealer products may be evaluated in a future expansion, but at launch the report focuses on: is sealing required? How often? What type? What happens if it fails?

## 4. MISLABELED STONE RULE (Named Rule)

### The Problem
The natural stone industry frequently mislabels marble and dolomitic marble as "quartzite" or "soft quartzite." This creates a material misrepresentation where the buyer believes they are getting a hard, etch-resistant surface (quartzite, Mohs 7) when they are actually getting a soft, etch-susceptible surface (marble, Mohs 3-5). The term "soft quartzite" does not exist as a geological category. It is a marketing fiction.

### The Rule
If the Consensus Bot or Evaluator Bot encounters ANY of the following, the material is reclassified as **marble/dolomitic marble** and evaluated under the Marble Material Class Profile:
- The term "soft quartzite" in any product description or spec sheet
- Any stone from the Known Mislabeled Stones list (below)
- Any stone that is marketed as quartzite but shows acid sensitivity/etching in testing or professional reports

### Known Mislabeled Stones (initial list — grows as category matures)
| Stone Name | Commonly Mislabeled As | Actual Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Fantasy Brown (also: Mascavo, Canyon Dawn) | Quartzite, Granite | Dolomitic Marble |
| Super White | Quartzite | Dolomitic Marble |
| Shadow Storm | Quartzite | Dolomitic Marble |

**This list will expand.** Every countertop evaluation where the bot encounters a stone marketed as quartzite should verify classification. The glass scratch test and acid etch test are the definitive diagnostics — quartzite scratches glass and does not etch from acid; marble does not scratch glass and etches from acid.

### Scoring Impact
Mislabeling is a **Yellow Finding** in the report. The stone itself is not penalized for being marble — it's evaluated honestly as marble. The finding reads:

*"[Stone name] is marketed as quartzite but is classified by geologists and stone professionals as dolomitic marble. This is a common industry mislabeling. As marble, this stone is softer (Mohs 3-5 vs quartzite's 7), susceptible to acid etching, and requires different maintenance expectations than true quartzite. This does not make it a bad stone — dolomitic marbles like Fantasy Brown are harder than typical marble and can perform well with proper care. However, the buyer should understand they are purchasing marble performance characteristics, not quartzite performance characteristics."*

If the mislabeling originates from the builder's spec sheet (builder says "quartzite countertops" and delivers Fantasy Brown), this elevates to a **stronger Yellow Finding** because the builder is setting incorrect expectations.

## 5. MATERIAL SAFETY — COUNTERTOP-SPECIFIC RULES

### 5.1 Core Framework
- Follow the Material Safety Investigation Sequence (Section 3 of bible)
- Search by product name first, not by material decomposition
- Manufacturing vs Consumer Hazard Rule applies — silicosis from stone/quartz cutting is excluded from scoring; only occupant exposure in the installed home is scored
- Certification hierarchy: C2C Material Health > Declare > Greenguard Gold > CARB Phase 2

### 5.2 Greenguard Certification — What It Measures and What It Doesn't

**How Greenguard works:** Products placed in sealed, temperature/humidity-controlled stainless steel chambers for 1-2 weeks. VOC emissions measured against thresholds.

**Two tiers:**
- Greenguard: ≤500 μg/m³ total VOCs
- Greenguard Gold: ≤220 μg/m³ total VOCs + formaldehyde ≤7.3 ppb + complies with California CDPH Section 01350

**Tests:** 360+ individual VOCs. Annual retesting required.
**Does NOT test:** Semi-VOCs (phthalates, biocides, flame retardants), heavy metals.
**Critical limitation:** Pass/fail only. Does not disclose exact emission levels. A product at 5 μg/m³ and one at 219 μg/m³ both pass Greenguard Gold.

**Countertop brands with Greenguard Gold:** Cambria (+ NSF 51), Silestone (+ NSF), Caesarstone, HanStone (+ NSF 51), Viatera/LG Hausys (+ NSF), Vicostone (+ NSF), Formica laminate, Everform/Formica solid surface, Corian.

**Scoring implication:** Greenguard Gold present = strong baseline (7-8.5 range depending on other factors). No Greenguard when competitors carry it = score lower. Greenguard Gold alone does not guarantee 9-10 because pass/fail limitation + no semi-VOC coverage.

### 5.3 PFAS in Stone Sealers — The Consumer Exposure Pathway Most Content Ignores

Most commercial stone sealers use fluoropolymer chemistry containing PFAS. Habitable (formerly Healthy Building Network) flagged this in 2016. EPA study (2009) found high PFAS levels in stone/tile/wood sealers. Sealers are applied to FOOD PREPARATION SURFACES and reapplied every 1-3 years. PFAS are persistent, bioaccumulative, linked to hormone disruption, immune effects, cancer risk.

**Habitable's Informed countertop guidance recommends:** Choose materials that don't require sealing. If natural stone, choose dense varieties. If sealing required, specify PFAS-free.

**Confirmed PFAS-free sealers:** TriNova Granite Sealer (confirmed by Green Science Policy Institute, 0-VOC, food-safe), Party Foul Sealer (PFAS-free, water-based), walnut oil/carnauba wax (natural, works on darker stones, 5-7 day cure).

**Scoring rules:**
- No sealing needed = no PFAS concern. Unaffected.
- Sealing needed + PFAS-free sealer specified = minor consideration, managed.
- Sealing needed + standard sealer assumed = Yellow Finding. PFAS on food-contact surface.
- Sealing needed + builder claims no sealing for porous stone = compounded Yellow Finding.

### 5.4 Antimicrobial Marketing Claims

Silestone's Microban and similar antimicrobial marketing = no positive score benefit. CDC and FDA do not recommend antimicrobial-treated building products for residential settings. Habitable advises against. No evidence of health benefit. Not a score penalty either — just no credit.

### 5.5 Low-Silica Quartz — Manufacturing vs Consumer Distinction

Cosentino HybriQ+ reduces silica to <40% (or <10% in XM lines). Caesarstone developing similar. This is a MANUFACTURING safety innovation. Does NOT affect consumer Material Safety score. Installed quartz safe regardless of silica content. Bot does NOT score high-silica lower than low-silica on consumer safety. Ethical dimension of brand choice = NOTES item, not score item.

### 5.6 Recycled Content Caution

Recycled content claims receive no automatic positive score. Old CRTs (lead-containing glass) sometimes used as recycled content. If recycled source is transparent and verified = neutral note. If unspecified = no score benefit.

### 5.7 Material-Specific Safety Profiles

**Engineered Quartz:** Resin VOC off-gassing is ACTIVE CONCERN. Greenguard Gold = strong evidence of managed emissions. No Greenguard when competitors carry it = lower score. Silicosis = manufacturing, excluded.

**Granite:** Radon = investigated, not on radar. SEALANT is the exposure pathway (PFAS). Dense granites needing no sealer = Material Safety advantage.

**Quartzite:** Stone = established safe. Sealant = PFAS concern per Section 5.3.

**Marble:** Stone = established safe. Higher sealing frequency than granite = higher cumulative PFAS exposure potential.

**Solid Surface — Acrylic:** Low-risk once cured. No sealing = no PFAS. Greenguard Gold on major brands. Pure acrylic scores higher than polyester blends (styrene in polyester).

**Sintered Stone / Porcelain:** No resin, no sealer, mineral composition. Cleanest profile alongside stainless steel.

**Laminate:** Substrate formaldehyde contained by laminate surface (IKEA Paradox). CARB Phase 2 compliance is key. NAF substrate = best case. Formica carries Greenguard Gold.

**Butcher Block:** Solid wood = safe. Adhesive system and finish are exposure pathways. Food-safe oil = no concern. Film finish = VOC during cure only.

**Concrete:** Concrete = safe. SEALER is the pathway. Same PFAS concern as stone, potentially worse due to higher porosity.

**Stainless Steel:** Inert. No pathways. Highest confidence alongside sintered stone.

### 5.8 Material Safety Baseline Orientation

| Material Class | Baseline Range | Key Variable |
|---|---|---|
| Stainless Steel | 8-10 | Substrate (usually no pathway) |
| Sintered Stone / Porcelain | 8-10 | No resin, no sealer |
| Natural Stone (no sealer needed) | 8-9 | Dense enough to skip sealing |
| Butcher Block (food-safe oil) | 7-9 | Finish type and adhesive |
| Engineered Quartz (Greenguard Gold) | 7-8.5 | Resin concern managed by cert |
| Solid Surface — Acrylic (Greenguard Gold) | 7-8.5 | Low-risk resin, no sealer |
| Natural Stone (requires sealing) | 5-7 | PFAS in sealer is the swing |
| Solid Surface — Polyester | 6-7.5 | Styrene in resin |
| Engineered Quartz (no Greenguard) | 5-7 | Resin concern without evidence |
| Laminate (CARB Phase 2) | 6-7 | Formaldehyde containment |
| Concrete | 4-8 | Entirely depends on sealer |
| Laminate (unknown substrate) | 4-6 | Formaldehyde uncertain |
| Engineered Quartz (opaque mfg) | 4-6 | Unknown resin, no cert, no transparency |
