The six-panel design represents the dominant aesthetic configuration in the Reliabilt steel lineup, with panel pressing creating geometric articulation on both door faces through conventional door manufacturing techniques[1][3][13][32]. The panel design itself serves both aesthetic and structural functions, with the pressed panel geometry contributing to bending stiffness in the door slab structure. The specific interior construction method distinguishes the Reliabilt steel doors from higher-specification commercial applications. Rather than welded edge construction or fully welded perimeter frames, the Reliabilt steel doors employ a bonded-frame construction methodology where the perimeter steel edge is adhesively bonded to the galvanized steel skin panels[47]. This construction approach reduces manufacturing cost and weight compared to fully welded perimeter frames, but creates potential failure modes under significant impact loading where the adhesive bond at the door perimeter can delaminate. Field reports document instances where significant impact loads—such as forced entry attempts or accidental heavy impacts—result in separation of the steel edge from the door skin, particularly in the lower hinge area where door weight and entry force concentrate[9][47].

Reliabilt steel entry doors incorporate a three-point locking system as standard on multiple residential models[5][26], representing a significant security enhancement compared to single-point deadbolt configurations[23]. The three-point locking design engages bolts at the top, middle, and bottom of the door frame simultaneously through activation of the primary locking mechanism, dispersing entry force across multiple mounting points rather than concentrating force on a single strike location[23].

The multi-point locking configuration provides measurable security advantages under forced-entry conditions. When compared to single-point deadbolt systems, multi-point locks distribute applied force across the door frame through three vertical bolts positioned to anchor into the door frame with vertical and horizontal engagement surfaces[23].
