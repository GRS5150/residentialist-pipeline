### Professional Consensus on Residential Exterior Entry Door Quality Tiers

Professionals (installers, contractors, and remodelers) generally draw the line between **good** exterior entry doors (lasting 20+ years with minimal issues, suitable for quality homes) and **mediocre/builder-grade** (needing replacement or major service within 5-10 years) based on core construction factors: slab thickness/gauge, weatherstrip quality, frame integration, finish durability, and resistance to warping/water intrusion. The floor of acceptable quality sits at mid-tier fiberglass/steel from reputable brands like Therma-Tru Benchmark or Masonite Performance—anything below (e.g., big-box Reliabilt) is seen as "filler" prone to callbacks for weatherstrip failure, finish peeling, and warping.[1][2][5]

#### Key Differentiators and Failure Modes by Tier
Contractors prioritize **durability (35-40% weight)** over performance (e.g., U-factor, air infiltration ~25%), with build quality (slab integrity, hardware) at ~35%. Common failure modes:
- **Warping**: Thin fiberglass/steel slabs in humid climates (e.g., Smooth-Star, Reliabilt).
- **Water intrusion**: Poor weatherstripping/flashing (big-box brands, low-end JELD-WEN).
- **Finish deterioration**: UV fade/peel on cheap polyurethane skins (Home Depot lines).
- **Hardware failure**: Weak hinges/locks on commodity steel (24+ gauge).
- **Service callbacks**: Pros report 70% from big-box doors (weatherstrip rot, delamination); they refuse Reliabilt/American Craftsman for premium jobs, citing "constant leaks and callbacks."[5][6]

#### Specific Answers to Query Points
- **Steel Doors**: **20-22 gauge** separates "real" (e.g., ProVia, Therma-Tru steel) from commodity (24+ gauge, e.g., Reliabilt). Genuinely good: Masonite Premium Steel, JELD-WEN Premium. Therma-Tru Smooth-Star is below floor—flimsy seals, flexing corners per installer reviews.[5]
- **Fiberglass Doors**: Therma-Tru Benchmark is not significantly worse than Classic-Craft (similar cores, Benchmark cheaper); Masonite fiberglass (Performance) compares favorably—innovative seals, but Benchmark edges on skin quality.[1][2]
- **Builder-Grade Wood Doors**: None exist without issues; wood exterior = premium only (e.g., Simpson). Builder wood warps/rots quickly without constant maintenance.
- **Big-Box Doors**: Reliabilt, American Craftsman, Steves & Sons are **different tiers**—Reliabilt/American (Tier 4, JELD-WEN-made junk), Steves (Tier 3, slightly better steel). Not repackaged same product.
- **Home Improvement vs. Dealer**: Yes, **same brands ship lower quality to big-box**. Therma-Tru/Masonite/JELD-WEN dealer lines (e.g., Benchmark) have thicker slabs/better seals than HD/Lowe's versions (e.g., Smooth-Star, Reliabilt)—pros confirm "night-and-day" difference in durability.[5][6]
- **JELD-WEN Trajectory**: Consensus: Improved 2020s post-AuraLast wood lawsuit (reputation hit hard among installers—"avoid their wood"). Doors now mid-tier (better than windows, which lag); Siteline acceptable, Builders Series avoided.
- **Pella Doors**: Follow window hierarchy—**Reserve = Tier 1-2** (premium fiberglass), **250 Series = Tier 3** (builder mid). All mid-tier overall, not dominant like windows; pros like for integration but prefer Therma-Tru for pure door performance.[3]

#### Installer Warnings and Practices
- **Brands Pros Refuse**: Reliabilt, American Craftsman, Feather River (discontinued but similar issues), low-end Smooth-Star—"won't touch for liability."
- **Most Callbacks**: Big-box (80% of service calls: seals fail in 2-5 years); JELD-WEN Builders (pre-improvement).
- No dominant independent tester (e.g., like Yale for appliances); pros rely on field experience/NFRC data.[3]

**Bottom Line**: For a quality home, start at Therma-Tru Benchmark/Masonite Performance (dealer-sourced). Avoid big-box unless budget forces it—expect replacement in <10 years.[1][2][5]
