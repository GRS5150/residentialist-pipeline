## Weathersealing System and Infiltration Performance

The Classic-Craft weathersealing architecture employs what Therma-Tru designates as a "medium-reach weatherstrip" system as the primary dynamic seal component, complemented by 7-shape corner seal pads positioned at the bottom corners of the door frame where sill and jamb members intersect[1][1][42]. The weatherstripping is specified as kerf-applied (meaning it is designed to insert into a precision groove or kerf in the door frame jamb), constructed from flexible foam-filled material with a polyethylene/urethane composite casing that provides durability and moisture resistance[23]. The medium-reach profile is "most commonly installed on the hinge side of the door" according to manufacturer guidance, suggesting a two-piece weatherstripping approach where differing profiles are deployed on load-bearing hinge stiles versus compression-loaded latch sides[23].

The manufacturer documentation confirms that genuine Therma-Tru weatherstripping is "specifically engineered in a variety of profiles to provide the best possible fit with Therma-Tru door systems, helping to deliver a precise seal between the door and frame"[23]. ## Durability Profile and Field Performance Assessment

The manufacturer markets Classic-Craft doors as "rot-proof" with claims that the fiberglass slab construction will "not dent or rust, nor will it warp, split, crack or rot," with the assertion that "wood doors and steel doors may weaken over time with exposure to the elements"[38]. This marketing positioning emphasizes relative durability advantages of fiberglass compared to traditional materials. However, field reports from practitioner communities document significant durability concerns that require careful evaluation. Forum reports from Fine Homebuilding contributors indicate that jamb stock "made out of finger-jointed white pine" deteriorates "where it meets the aluminum threshold after a few years of service"[18][18], directly contradicting marketing claims of comprehensive rot-resistance.

A separate field report documents a case where a Classic-Craft door slab was replaced twice, suggesting delamination or structural failure of the slab itself in that instance[19][19]. The practitioner commentary in that case attributed the failures to heat trapping caused by installation behind a full glass storm door, explaining that "it traps heat (think of your car on a summer day) & cooks the plastic surrounds, fiberglass, whatever"[19][19]. The thermal expansion mechanism is detailed: "the inner skin of the door & the outer skin of the door are separate & are subject to different rates of thermal expansion. The skin behind the storm door can easily be 30 degrees warmer than the surface inside the house. That skin will grow with the expansion causing the door to bow & pull away from the weather stripping"[19][19]. This technical explanation indicates that fiberglass doors are subject to differential thermal expansion stresses that can compromise weathersealing integrity when exposed to extreme temperature differentials.

The warranty documentation explicitly excludes coverage for doors installed behind storm doors, with notation that "The reason is it traps heat (think of your car on a summer day) & cooks the plastic surrounds, fiberglass, whatever"[19][19]. This exclusion is noteworthy because it represents an installation context that homeowners might reasonably pursue for energy-efficiency purposes, yet the manufacturer does not warrant the door under such conditions. The implication is that the fiberglass slab and compression weatherstripping system are subject to degradation under the thermal cycling and differential expansion stresses imposed by storm door installation.

More recent BBB complaint records document cases involving Classic-Craft French doors with sidelights purchased in June 2025 that exhibited "doors leaking, mold growing over outside surfaces, wood rot" by November 2025—a five-month failure timeline[24]. Another complaint references a door marketed as having a "lifetime warranty stating that the door will 'never rot like wood,' instead, the interior of the door is rotted, identical to wood," indicating that foam core delamination or water intrusion is occurring despite the lifetime slab warranty[31].

Finish durability relates to the PrismaGuard premium stain or paint finish applied by Therma-Tru at the factory. The warranty provides a 10-year transferable coverage for this applied finish, with the balance transferable to a second owner[1][1][1]. Normal maintenance procedures require reapplication of topcoat every 3-5 years without requiring full stripping and restaining[37]. The staining/painting process must occur within specific timeframes—fiberglass door systems must be finished within 6 months of installation date to maintain warranty coverage, while bare or unprotected wood surfaces must be primed and painted or stained and top-coated within the lesser of 2 weeks of installation or exposure to weather[34][34]. All six surfaces of the door must be properly finished[34]. Finish preparation work involves specific temperature and humidity constraints: work temperatures must be between 50°F and 90°F with humidity less than 85%, and work should not occur in direct sunlight[37].

## Warranty Structure and Transferability

The Classic-Craft warranty architecture provides a transferable lifetime limited warranty on the door slab and structural components, transferable one time to a new owner should the original homeowner sell their home[1][1][1][1]. This represents the manufacturer's most comprehensive warranty offering. The PrismaGuard premium finish receives a separate 10-year limited warranty that is also transferable, with "the balance of this finish warranty" transferring to "a second owner"[1][1][1].

The detailed warranty documentation specifies that the warranty covers "NON-GLASS COMPONENTS" as "free from non-conformities in material and workmanship"[34]. Glass components receive separate coverage against "non-conformities resulting in internal glazing failure, seal failure, internal insert slippage, and permanent and material visual obstruction from moisture or dust film formation in the air space of the sealed glass unit"[34]. Hinges in fiberglass products and multi-point locking systems are also warranted "against non-conformities in the mechanical and" operational performance[34].

Critical limitations apply to warranty coverage. The warranty "applies only when all of these parts are genuine Therma-Tru components"—meaning that substitution of non-Therma-Tru components voids warranty protection[34]. The warranty explicitly excludes coverage for "rotting, splitting, warping, swelling, or other adverse condition" of wood components "of or attributed to or" improper installation, with specific exclusions for "improper pre-installation storage, including inadequate shelter or inadequate venting of shipping wrap in humid locations" and "misapplication of Products or faulty building design or construction, including" various installation defects[34]. Additionally, the warranty does not cover "product reinstalled after removal from its original installation, except in connection with proper and timely maintenance of components which incur normal wear and tear, such as the weatherstrip, door bottom sweep (gasket), and corner seal pads"[34]. Labor costs are explicitly excluded: "Labor for removing, installing, or replacing Product or components or labor for other materials that are removed, reinstalled, or refinished in conjunction with repairing or replacing the Product or component" is not covered[34].

A supplemental warranty offering—the Tru-Defense Door System Warranty Rider—provides additional protection with potential reimbursement "up to a maximum of $2,000" if "water infiltrates under a properly assembled, installed, and maintained fiberglass door system"[34]. This rider, however, requires proof of proper assembly, installation, and maintenance, and places the burden of demonstrating these conditions on the warranty claimant. The warranty rider specifically "does not warrant workmanship of anyone who assembles or installs a Tru-Defense door system, including Therma-Tru Certified Door System Installers, nor damages caused by improper handling, assembly, or installation"[17][17].

Warranty enforcement requires proof of purchase, photographs clearly showing the issue, photographs of the Therma-Tru watermark on glass and/or the Therma-Tru stamp on hinges, and relevant sizing information[17][17]. The warranty claim process is centrally administered, with homeowners directed to submit claims through Therma-Tru's formal warranty system.
