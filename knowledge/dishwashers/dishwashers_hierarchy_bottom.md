Professionals distinguish a **good residential dishwasher** from a mediocre one primarily by **tub material** (full stainless steel for better durability, drying, and hygiene vs. hybrid/plastic), **service rates** (under 10-15% in first 5 years per Yale data where available), **drying performance** (condensation or active systems that handle plastics effectively), and **long-term reliability** (fewer control board, pump, or leak issues per repair tech feedback). The floor of **acceptable quality for a quality home** starts at Bosch 300 Series or equivalent, with full stainless tubs, quiet operation (~44-46 dB), and proven low service needs; below this lies **builder-grade filler** like Bosch 100 Series or basic Whirlpool/Maytag/Amana models, which use plastic-hybrid tubs prone to cracking and poorer drying.[1][2][3][4][6]

### Bosch Series Breakdown (Professional Consensus)
Bosch dominates the "good enough" line due to superior engineering, with repair techs and Yale praising low service rates (~5-8% for 300+ series). Consumer Reports (CR) rates them highly for washing but notes drying limitations in lower tiers.[4][6]
- **Bosch 300 Series**: Sits firmly on the **good side**—first "true Bosch" with **full stainless tub**, RackMatic adjustability, 44-46 dB noise, Speed60 cycle, and better drying via stainless heat retention. Professionals call it "great value/solid performance" for quality homes; Yale service rates low (~7%). Kitchen designers recommend it as entry deluxe.[1][2][3][4][6]
- **Bosch 100 Series**: **Mediocre/builder-grade**—hybrid tub (plastic bottom), 48-50 dB noise (noticeable in open kitchens), basic cycles, lighter build. Techs note it's "not the same quality" as 300+; suitable only for budgets but professionals warn against for longevity (higher leak/drying complaints). CR says it washes well but lacks features.[1][2][3][4][6]

Higher Bosch (500/800) exceed "good enough" with AutoAir drying and 38-44 dB, but 300 is the value floor.[2][3][4]
