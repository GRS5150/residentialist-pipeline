## THE "REPEL" TECHNOLOGY: WHAT IT ACTUALLY DOES

**Marketing Claim:** "Water-resistant engineered hardwood."

**Engineering Reality:**
- Repel = **edge sanding + surface treatment** (hydrophobic coating on exposed surfaces, primarily edges).
- NOT core waterproofing or structural moisture resistance beyond standard engineered hardwood.
- **Functional effect:** 
  - Reduced edge swelling/cupping if minor water exposure occurs (spill cleanup, bathroom humidity).
  - Extended time to drying before delamination risk.
  - Estimated extra tolerance: ~5-10 minutes vs. standard engineered (not waterproof).

**Comparison to Bruce HydroPel:**
- **Bruce HydroPel:** Similar concept — edge treatment + surface hydrophobic finish.
- **Competitive positioning:** Equivalent technology; marketing differentiation rather than structural superiority.
- Both brands in same tier; Repel slightly more aggressive marketing ("water-resistant" vs. HydroPel's "moisture-resistant").

**Honest assessment:** Repel is engineered hardwood with **enhanced edge protection**, not true "water-resistant hardwood." Suitable for kitchens/baths with standard precautions (wipe spills promptly); not suitable for below-grade or high-moisture environments (use vinyl/LVP for those).
