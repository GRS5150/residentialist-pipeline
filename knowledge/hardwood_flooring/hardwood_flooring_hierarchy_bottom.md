### Professional Hierarchy of Hardwood Flooring Brands

Professional installers and flooring experts distinguish **good hardwood** (precise milling, durable finishes, reliable long-term performance with refinishing potential and stability) from **mediocre** (inconsistent milling, thin veneers prone to delamination/cupping, short warranties) primarily by veneer thickness (minimum 2-3mm sawn for engineered), core construction (multi-ply birch vs. HDF), finish quality (aluminum oxide or nano-tech vs. basic UV), and milling precision (tight T&G fit to prevent gapping).[1][2][3] The **floor of acceptable quality** sits at brands with NWFA/NOFMA certification, CARB II low-formaldehyde compliance, and at least 2mm sawn veneer or solid 3/4" planks—installers won't stake their reputation on sub-1.5mm rotary-peeled imports or house brands like LifeProof, which they view as "builder-grade filler" unfit for quality homes due to poor refinishing and moisture issues.[2][3]

#### Brand-Specific Insights
- **Shaw**: Big distributor with solid warranties, but pros rank mid-tier—**good for volume, not precision**; Repel line (2mm sawn) holds up, but basic lines gap/cup.[1]
- **Bruce/AHF**: **Legacy declining**—once solid, now mediocre with 1.2mm rotary veneers prone to delamination/peeling; installers note inconsistent milling.[1]
- **Armstrong**: Bottom mid-tier; fading reputation, basic finishes wear out.[1]
- **Mohawk/RevWood**: Reliable mid-tier, versatile; pros like sustainability, but not premium.[1]
- **Mannington/Mullican**: **Genuine mid-tier**—decent for value homes, but aspirational claims exceed performance (minor gapping).[1]
- **Columbia/Anderson Tuftex/Bella Cera**: Variable; pros avoid due to finish issues, thin stock.
- **LifeProof**: **Marketing gimmick**—not true hardwood (thin laminate-like engineered); installers refuse for poor stability.[2]
- **Chinese imports**: **Universal red flag**—<1mm rotary veneer misrepresented as engineered; high delam/cupping, formaldehyde concerns; pros assess as disposable.[2]

#### Key Thresholds and Warnings
- **Veneer thickness**: Pros demand **2mm sawn minimum** for "real" engineered (refinishable); below (e.g., 1mm rotary) = no-go due to delam risk.[2][3]
- **T&G milling problems**: Bruce/AHF, Chinese imports (loose fits cause gapping).[3]
- **Formaldehyde**: Chinese/LifeProof raise flags (non-CARB II); top tiers (Mirage, Morningstar) are formaldehyde-free.[2]
- **Brands pros warn against**: LifeProof, cheap Chinese, low-end Bruce—dread for callbacks; designers refuse sub-2mm or non-sawn.[1][2][3]
- **Good enough for quality home**: Tier 2+ (e.g., Somerset up); below is "filler" lacking refinishing/durability.[3]
