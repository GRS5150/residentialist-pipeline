### Company Background
Kraus USA operates as a pure importer and marketeer headquartered in Port Washington, NY, with no manufacturing facilities [SRC-005, SRC-010]. The company sources from 3-4 unidentified Chinese factories depending on product line, with no design engineering or manufacturing control [SRC-001, SRC-005]. Kraus does not disclose specific factory names or locations, operating as a middleman between Chinese manufacturers and US consumers [SRC-010]. The business model represents a step above commodity Amazon flooding but remains well below brands with manufacturing accountability [SRC-005]. Financial health and ownership structure are not disclosed, typical of import-focused operations in this market segment.

### Strengths
- **Legitimate Certification**: Full UPC, cUPC, NSF/ANSI 61, NSF 372, and ASME A112.18.1 compliance makes this legally installable contraband-free product [SRC-006]

### Deficiencies
- **Cartridge Lottery System**: Customers cannot determine whether their faucet contains premium Kerox/Flühs cartridges or lower-tier alternatives, with Kraus refusing to disclose cartridge suppliers by model [SRC-001, SRC-002]
- **Limited Service Life**: Five-year cartridge warranty and parts availability guarantee create long-term serviceability risk compared to lifetime warranties from established manufacturers [SRC-003, SRC-007]

### What You Should Know
Kraus occupies the bottom tier of the legitimate faucet market — certified and legal, but with significant compromises. The most problematic aspect is their "cartridge lottery" where some faucets contain world-class Hungarian Kerox or German Flühs cartridges while others use lower-tier Chinese components, with no way for customers to know which they're purchasing. This creates an inherent quality gamble at point of sale.

The company's pure marketeer business model means no manufacturing accountability or quality control beyond what their unnamed Chinese suppliers provide. While the solid brass construction and certifications meet basic requirements, the five-year warranty limitations on critical components and parts availability represent meaningful long-term risks compared to established brands offering lifetime coverage.
