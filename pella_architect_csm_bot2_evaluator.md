## SCORE JUSTIFICATION

**Paragraph 1 — Quality context:**
The Pella Architect Series / Reserve earns a Quality score of 7.0 (B-), which represents a genuine premium above the baseline for aluminum-clad wood construction but falls short of the scores achievable by products with full component transparency. Three deterministic Quality components — spacer system, weatherstripping attachment, and glazing bead construction — score at the rubric default of 5 due to Pella's non-disclosure of these specifications in any public-facing or professional documentation. This is not assumed deficiency; the performance data (CR 61, AI 0.05 cfm/ft²) is strongly consistent with a warm-edge spacer system, and a confirmed warm-edge classification would raise Quality by approximately +0.25 points to approximately 7.25 (B). The premium characteristics of the Architect/Reserve line — extruded aluminum cladding (vs. roll-form on the Lifestyle Series), EnduraGuard post-cut wood immersion treatment, four-point fusion welding on Reserve products, through-stile construction, and premium hardware partnerships (Baldwin, Saldo, Roto) — are reflected in the judgment-based elements of 1A and 1B, which score in the 7.5–8.5 range. Professional consensus (1C = 6.0, Mixed tier) is the other significant Quality suppressor: the only Tier 1 source (Green Building Advisor) provides a mixed-to-negative assessment, describing the product as "not competitive with Marvin Elevate, Sierra Pacific, or fiberglass at the premium tier," and the documented class action litigation history — even if primarily against predecessor products — appropriately weighs on quality perception.

**Paragraph 2 — Durability context:**
Durability scores 7.8 (B), reflecting a product with strong warranty terms and good physical longevity expectations that are partially undermined by documented failure-mode history and a repairability profile constrained by Pella's warranty claims experience. The Warranty-Lifespan Alignment Rule (Universal Principle 6) was triggered: the deterministic warranty score (9.5) exceeds the judgment-based longevity score (7.5) by 2.0 points, meeting the 1.5-point threshold. The cap sets the deterministic warranty ceiling at 9.5 (judgment + 2), which in this case equals the original deterministic score — the rule triggered but did not reduce the score, reflecting that the warranty terms, while generous, are not wildly inconsistent with the 25–35 year field lifespan documented by GBA experts. The primary Durability concern is the documented wood rot class action against Architect/Designer Series predecessor products — a MDL-consolidated federal proceeding (2014) establishing systematic sill extrusion and gasket gap design defects. Pella has applied EnduraGuard treatment and revised sill geometry; these revisions are credible mitigants, but no independent Tier 1 or Tier 2 source has evaluated current (post-2012) production for resolution of the specific defects. Repairability and Support (6.4) is dragged down by three factors: (1) the documented warranty claim denial pattern from the class action record; (2) no formal parts availability commitment in Pella's documentation; and (3) partial uncertainty about IGU replacement method.

**Paragraph 3 — Performance context and overall position:**
Performance at 8.6 (A-) is the clear strength of this evaluation and represents one of the strongest dual-pane Performance scores in the calibration set. Two subscores reach 10.0: Air Infiltration (0.05 cfm/ft², a specific tested value — not a threshold — from WDMA Hallmark Certified data) and Structural (CW50 = PG50+ equivalent). Condensation Resistance scores 9.0 (CR 61). Together, these three subscores account for 55% of the Performance weight and all score 9–10. The drag comes from U-Factor (0.28 = score 7.0) and VT (0.48 = score 7.0), both reflecting the inherent tradeoff of multi-coat Low-E glazing. The overall score of 7.8 (B) sits at the top of the B band, approximately 0.2 points below B+. The primary gap between this result and B+ is Quality data opacity — specifically the three "unknown" component scores at 5 that reflect Pella's non-disclosure of spacer type, weatherstripping attachment method, and glazing bead construction. These are not assumed failures; they are scoring inputs that reflect the absence of confirmed data. A manufacturer that publishes these specifications would score meaningfully higher on Quality with no change to Performance or Durability.
