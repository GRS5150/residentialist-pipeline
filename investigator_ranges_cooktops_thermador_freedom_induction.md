### Platform Disclosure
BSH shared platform: Thermador shares core induction components (E.G.O. coils, Infineon IGBTs, Schott CERAN glass) with Bosch and Gaggenau. However, Freedom's full-surface hardware is genuinely unique — 48-56 element array with 8-16 independent inverter channels vs Bosch's 4-channel FlexInduction system. This complexity explains the 4.9% vs 1.7% Bosch service rate gap.

### Key Components
**Induction System:** Full-surface multi-element (48-56 individual 3" inductors) • **Coils:** E.G.O.-Elektro-Gerätebau (Germany) • **IGBTs:** Infineon HighSpeed3 H3 series • **Glass:** Schott CERAN premium grade • **Architecture:** Multi-channel inverter (8-16 independent channels)

### Strengths
- **Revolutionary cooking flexibility:** 48-56 element array eliminates fixed zones, allowing cookware placement anywhere on surface with 63% more effective cooking area than competitors
- **Premium component sourcing:** E.G.O. coils, Infineon IGBTs, and Schott CERAN glass represent best-in-class induction hardware
- **High-power performance:** 4600W boost zones with documented sub-2 minute boil times and accommodation for large rectangular griddles

### Deficiencies
- **Reliability complexity penalty:** 4.9% service rate is 3x higher than Bosch induction (1.7%) due to multi-channel electronics creating more failure points
- **Touchscreen interface problems:** 6.5" TFT screen suffers from lag, grease/water unresponsiveness, and system freezes requiring breaker resets
- **Power board repair economics:** Multi-channel inverter failures cost $400-$800+ to repair, approaching 30-40% of replacement cost

### What You Should Know
This is the only true full-surface induction cooktop in residential markets — you can place cookware literally anywhere on the glass. The technology is genuinely revolutionary, but you pay a reliability penalty for the complexity. The 4.9% service rate reflects the engineering challenge of coordinating 48-56 independent elements through sophisticated power electronics. Ensure your cookware is induction-compatible (magnetic bottom test). Budget for potential power board repairs in years 3-5, as the multi-channel inverter is the most expensive component to replace.

### Repair Economics
**Most common:** Power board/inverter failure (multi-channel complexity), touchscreen issues, pan detection errors. **Parts cost:** Power board $400-$800+, touchscreen module $200-$400. **Total installed:** $600-$1200 for major repairs. At this price point ($4000-$5000 MSRP), repairs under $2000 are cost-justified, but power board failures approach the replacement threshold.
