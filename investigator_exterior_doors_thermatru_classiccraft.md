### Company Background
Therma-Tru operates as a subsidiary of Fortune Brands Home & Security, a publicly traded company (NYSE: FBHS) with manufacturing concentrated at their Butler, Indiana facility [SRC-013]. Fortune Brands is a diversified home products conglomerate that also owns Moen plumbing and Master Lock, providing substantial financial backing and integrated manufacturing capacity. Therma-Tru has operated as the premium door brand within Fortune Brands' portfolio since acquisition, maintaining dedicated manufacturing rather than sharing production lines with lower-tier brands. The company benefits from Fortune Brands' stable financial position and established distribution network, though recent corporate restructuring has focused on maximizing margins across all subsidiaries.

### Strengths
- **Thermal Engineering Excellence**: Polyurethane foam core with K-factor of 0.15 delivers superior insulation compared to polystyrene alternatives, achieving NFRC-certified U-factors as low as 1.19 [SRC-020, SRC-002]
- **Comprehensive Hardware Integration**: Multi-point locking system with ball-bearing hinges and advanced weatherstripping creates professional-grade security and sealing performance [SRC-009, SRC-010, SRC-011]

### Deficiencies
- **Jamb System Vulnerability**: Finger-jointed white pine jamb stock deteriorates at aluminum threshold interface within years, contradicting rot-resistant marketing claims [SRC-004]
- **Thermal Expansion Failures**: Documented slab replacement requirements due to differential thermal expansion between inner and outer skins, particularly behind storm doors [SRC-005]

### What You Should Know
This door represents premium engineering with genuine thermal performance advantages, but requires careful attention to installation context and maintenance scheduling. The lifetime slab warranty provides significant value, but excludes storm door installations and coastal applications—two common scenarios where failures have been documented. The PrismaGuard finish requires topcoat renewal every 3-5 years under specific temperature and humidity conditions, making this unsuitable for low-maintenance applications.

Multiple BBB complaints document rapid deterioration timelines as short as five months, suggesting that while the door slab itself may be durable, the complete door system faces real-world challenges that warranty exclusions don't adequately address. Professional contractors specifically cite jamb failures that contradict marketing promises, indicating a gap between engineered performance and field reality.

### Repair Economics
Most common failure involves weatherstripping breakdown requiring field-replaceable components at moderate cost, but jamb deterioration requires complete frame replacement at $800-1200 including labor, making prevention through proper installation critical for cost justification.
