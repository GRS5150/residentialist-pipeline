### Strengths
- **Material leadership**: Silgranit II formula with 80% natural granite and 35+ patents represents the composite sink benchmark, outperforming generic composite materials
- **Heat performance**: Independently verified 536°F (280°C) heat resistance exceeds most kitchen demands and surpasses competing composite materials
- **Maintenance advantage**: Matte finish actively hides water spots and fingerprints while metal transfer marks are easily removable with mild abrasives

### Deficiencies
- **Ecosystem lock-in**: Requires Blanco-branded accessories with limited aftermarket options, increasing long-term ownership costs
- **Heirloom factor**: Composite material lacks the generational character and repair tradition of fireclay or cast iron alternatives
- **Market premium**: As the original composite apron-front sink, commands higher pricing than newer market entrants

### What You Should Know
This is the composite farmhouse sink that created the category. Blanco's Silgranit formula genuinely delivers on durability promises — 5-year user reports show zero scratches, stains, or chips. The matte finish is brilliantly practical, hiding every water spot and fingerprint that would show on stainless steel. Metal marks from pots are cosmetic and wipe away easily. You're buying 25-50 years of genuinely low-maintenance performance from a financially stable German company.

### Repair Economics
Composite sinks rarely need repair — the primary maintenance cost is specialized cleaners for metal transfer marks (Bar Keepers Friend, ~$5 annually). Accessories are brand-locked to Blanco ecosystem.

### Score Justification
The 83/100 reflects Blanco's material engineering leadership and proven long-term performance, but stops short of Tier 1 due to ecosystem limitations and the inherent trade-offs of choosing composite over traditional materials. This is excellence within the composite category, not perfection across all sink types.
