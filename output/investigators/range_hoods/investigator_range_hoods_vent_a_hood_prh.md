### Company Background
Vent-A-Hood is a family-owned company founded in 1933 and still operating from Houston, Texas. The company has maintained continuous ownership and manufacturing in the same location for 90+ years, representing exceptional stability in the appliance industry. They manufacture exclusively in Houston and hold patents on their proprietary Magic Lung centrifugal blower system that no other manufacturer uses. Professional consensus positions them as "the Sub-Zero of range hoods" — the heritage benchmark brand that kitchen designers specify when ventilation quality is the primary concern rather than cost optimization.

### Strengths
- **Centrifugal Grease Capture System**: The Magic Lung blower spins grease out before it reaches the fan mechanism, preventing the motor contamination that degrades traditional filter-based hoods [SRC-002]
- **Motor Longevity**: Multiple repair technicians report seeing original motors still functioning after 30+ years due to the grease-free centrifugal design [SRC-004]

### Deficiencies
- **CFM Limitations**: Kitchen designers note that Vent-A-Hood's internal blower design delivers lower maximum CFM than external-blower setups, potentially inadequate for very high-BTU commercial ranges [SRC-010]
- **Price Premium**: Typical PRH models cost $2,500-4,000+ versus $200-800 for comparable-CFM alternatives from Broan or Zephyr [SRC-008]

### What You Should Know
The Vent-A-Hood PRH represents a fundamentally different approach to kitchen ventilation. Instead of using filters that allow grease to pass through and eventually coat your ductwork and motor, the Magic Lung system uses centrifugal force to capture grease before it reaches any mechanical components. This is why these units essentially last forever — there's no grease buildup to damage the motor bearings over time.

You're paying a significant premium for this architecture, but kitchen professionals consistently specify Vent-A-Hood when clients prioritize quiet operation, zero maintenance, and buy-it-once reliability. The trade-off is CFM capacity — if you're running a 60,000+ BTU commercial range, you may need an external blower system instead. For typical residential cooking, the PRH delivers professional-grade ventilation that will outlast your kitchen renovation.

Motor replacement is rare but costs $800-1,200. Given 30+ year motor longevity, repair costs are easily justified.
