### Company Background
Lennox International Inc. (NYSE: LII) is a publicly traded HVAC manufacturer founded in 1895, headquartered in Richardson, Texas. The company is profitable and financially stable, operating as a premium brand in the residential and commercial HVAC space. Lennox manufactures its own products in company-owned facilities and owns Armstrong Air (primarily air handlers), with some internal component sharing between divisions. Unlike many competitors, Lennox has not been recently acquired and maintains consistent ownership structure as an independent public company focused exclusively on climate control systems.

### Strengths
- **Industry-Leading Efficiency**: SEER2 ratings up to 28.0 represent the highest efficiency of any ducted residential split system, with ENERGY STAR Most Efficient designation [S1]
- **Premium Component Integration**: Copeland variable-speed scroll compressor with electronic expansion valve and variable-speed BLDC motors deliver superior performance and reliability [S2]

### Deficiencies
- **Proprietary Service Ecosystem**: Parts cost 15-20% more than competitors with Lennox-authorized distributor requirements and longer lead times [S3]
- **Warranty Service Challenges**: Contractor consensus rates Lennox warranty claims as most difficult to process, creating potential service delays [S3]

### What You Should Know
The SL28XCV represents a clear trade-off between upfront performance and long-term service economics. You're buying the most efficient ducted split system available, with genuinely impressive 28 SEER2 ratings that will deliver substantial energy savings over the system's lifetime. The engineering is genuinely premium — Copeland compressors, electronic expansion valves, and variable-speed motors throughout.

However, this efficiency comes with service cost penalties. The proprietary iComfort system locks you into Lennox thermostats and diagnostic tools, while parts cost 15-20% more than competitors with more restrictive distribution. Contractors consistently report this as the "best product with worst servicing economics." If you're a long-term homeowner prioritizing energy bills over service costs, this system is unmatched. If you're cost-conscious about repairs or plan to move within 5-7 years, the service premium may outweigh efficiency gains.

### Repair Economics
Most common failures involve electronic controls and variable-speed components, with proprietary parts requiring Lennox-authorized service. Expect 15-20% premium on parts costs and potential delays for specialized components like control boards and communicating system modules.
