### Company Background
Daltile is owned by Mohawk Industries (NYSE: MHK), the world's largest flooring company with over $10 billion in annual revenue [SRC-002]. Mohawk also owns Marazzi USA and American Olean, creating a tile manufacturing empire with multiple US plants and dominant market share in US distribution [SRC-002]. As the largest US tile manufacturer, Daltile benefits from extensive quality control infrastructure and unmatched logistics capabilities [SRC-005]. The company maintains multiple US manufacturing facilities plus qualifying partner facilities for production [SRC-001]. This is a publicly-traded corporate giant, not a boutique premium brand, which explains both the reliability and the value-oriented positioning.

### Strengths
- **Supply Chain Dominance**: Unmatched logistics, consistent dye lots, and guaranteed stock availability make it the default specification for large residential projects where reliability matters [SRC-003]
- **Manufacturing Consistency**: Extensive quality control infrastructure as the largest US manufacturer delivers consistent sizing and bonding performance that installers can rely on [SRC-004, SRC-005]

### Deficiencies
- **White Body Construction**: Shows chips at white substrate unlike through-body porcelain, particularly problematic in heavy traffic areas like doorways [SRC-004]
- **Slip Resistance Variability**: DCOF values drop below 0.42 on polished finishes, creating potential safety concerns in wet areas [SRC-001]

### What You Should Know
Daltile Panoramic represents the "reliable premium" choice in US porcelain tile — a step above big box store brands but positioned below true luxury imports like Porcelanosa. The rectified large format (24x48 and larger) provides the modern aesthetic you want, backed by Mohawk's massive distribution network that ensures you can get consistent batches for large projects.

The trade-off is straightforward: you're getting proven reliability and logistics rather than cutting-edge materials technology. The white body construction means chips will show, and the HD inkjet surface, while advanced, isn't the through-body construction found in premium alternatives. For most residential applications, these compromises are acceptable given the strong durability credentials and lifetime warranty.
