### Company Background
Merola Tile Corp is a New Jersey-based importer and distributor, not a manufacturer [SRC-005]. The company operates as an aggregator sourcing ceramic tile from multiple international manufacturing partners across Spain, Brazil, China, and Italy depending on production capacity and pricing [SRC-003, SRC-005]. Merola focuses on decorative patterns at affordable price points with primary distribution through Home Depot [SRC-001, SRC-005]. Quality control and warranty terms vary significantly by the individual OEM manufacturer since Merola doesn't control production [SRC-005]. This is essentially a private label importing operation that connects Home Depot customers with overseas tile production at competitive prices.

### Strengths
- **Attractive Decorative Patterns**: Wide range of encaustic-look, patterned cement-look, and decorative designs that are praised by customers for unique aesthetics at affordable pricing [SRC-001, SRC-004]

### Deficiencies
- **Inconsistent Quality Control**: As an importer aggregating from multiple OEMs, quality varies dramatically by source factory with consistent reports of dimensional variance, shade variation, and soft body clay that chips during installation [SRC-002, SRC-003, SRC-004]

### What You Should Know
Merola Tile represents the reality of big-box store tile: attractive patterns at budget prices, but with meaningful quality trade-offs. Professional installers consistently report sizing issues within boxes, shade variation between boxes from the same order, and soft ceramic bodies that chip during cutting. The glazing quality varies significantly depending on which overseas factory produced your specific batch.

This is builder-grade tile best suited for small decorative applications like bathroom accent walls or backsplashes where dimensional consistency matters less and traffic is minimal. For floors, especially in high-traffic areas, professionals consistently steer clients toward higher-grade options due to durability concerns and installation challenges.

### Repair Economics
Most common issue is premature glaze wear requiring full replacement since individual tiles cannot be color-matched due to shade variation between production runs; replacement cost typically $2-4 per square foot plus installation labor making repair uneconomical for large areas.
