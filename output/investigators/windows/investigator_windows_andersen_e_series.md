### Company Background
Andersen Corporation, founded in 1903 and still family-owned, is America's largest window manufacturer with multi-plant production facilities nationwide. The company operates multiple manufacturing sites to serve national distribution, which enables massive scale and availability but introduces minor quality variance risk compared to single-factory premium brands. Andersen has remained financially stable and profitable throughout its century-plus history, positioning the E-Series as their mid-tier offering below premium Marvin/Loewen but above builder-grade vinyl products.

### Strengths
- **Proven Longevity**: Frame construction delivers 30-40 year typical lifespan with wood interior protected by extruded aluminum cladding [SRC-003]
- **Energy Certification**: Achieves ENERGY STAR Most Efficient status, indicating top-tier thermal performance in the market

### Deficiencies
- **Mid-Range Structural Rating**: Water penetration rating of only DP25-35 falls well short of premium products that achieve DP50+ ratings [SRC-004]
- **IGU Seal Longevity Risk**: Insulating glass units show 15-25 year seal integrity with moderate failure risk, particularly on older aluminum spacer designs [SRC-003, SRC-004]

### What You Should Know
The E-Series represents Andersen's sweet spot for homeowners planning a 25-30 year hold, delivering proven durability and energy efficiency without premium pricing. The wood interior provides excellent thermal performance and repairability, while aluminum cladding protects against weather exposure. However, you're getting mid-tier construction quality—the structural ratings and seal systems don't match what you'd find in true premium lines.

Maintenance expectations are reasonable but real: plan for cladding paint refresh every 10-15 years and potential IGU replacement after 15-25 years. The multi-plant manufacturing means quality can vary slightly between production runs, unlike boutique single-factory brands. For most homeowners, this represents solid value engineering rather than cutting corners.

### Repair Economics
Most common failure is IGU seal degradation after 15-25 years, with replacement glass units typically costing $200-400 per window plus installation. Hardware components last 20-30 years and benefit from Andersen's extensive parts network. The wood interior allows for field repairs and refinishing, making long-term maintenance cost-effective compared to vinyl alternatives.

### Score Justification
The 85/100 score reflects strong durability performance carrying the highest weight, offset by mid-tier quality construction. While structural ratings and seal systems fall short of premium standards, proven longevity and energy efficiency justify the Tier 2 positioning.
