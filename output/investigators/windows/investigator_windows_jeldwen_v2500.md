### Company Background
JELD-WEN is a publicly-traded company (NYSE: JELD) founded in 1960, operating as one of North America's largest door and window manufacturers. The V-2500 vinyl line is manufactured across multiple plants, which has created documented quality variance issues. The company has faced class-action lawsuits related to seal failures and operates in the builder-grade market segment where cost optimization often takes precedence over premium construction methods. JELD-WEN serves both retail and contractor channels but positions the V-2500 as an entry-level offering for cost-conscious builds.

### Strengths
- **Fusion-Welded Construction**: Corners are fusion-welded rather than mechanically fastened, providing better structural integrity for the price point
- **Multi-Chamber Profile**: 4-5 chamber vinyl design offers reasonable insulation properties for builder-grade construction [SRC-002]

### Deficiencies
- **Thin Profile Construction**: Wall thickness of 0.072"-0.080" falls below premium standards (0.084"-0.090"), creating durability concerns [SRC-002]
- **No Exterior Cladding Protection**: Pure vinyl exterior provides no secondary weather barrier against UV and thermal cycling, creating significant durability liability in high-UV climates [SRC-003]
- **Basic Hardware Systems**: Coil-spring balance system typically fails within 12-18 years, and cam-lock systems with zinc alloy components present corrosion risks [SRC-009, SRC-010]

### What You Should Know
The V-2500 represents JELD-WEN's builder-grade vinyl offering designed primarily for cost-conscious new construction and replacement projects. While the windows feature fusion-welded corners and multi-chamber profiles, the thin vinyl construction and lack of exterior cladding protection make them vulnerable to UV degradation and thermal cycling damage over time.

Contractors commonly use these windows for cost builds, but industry professionals warn of seal failure rates accelerating around the 10-year mark. The coil-spring balance system will likely require replacement within 12-18 years, and the lack of ENERGY STAR certification means no eligibility for tax credits. If you're in a high-UV climate or seeking long-term durability, consider upgrading to a thicker-profile vinyl window or alternative cladding options.

### Repair Economics
Most common failure is seal degradation (10-year timeline) and coil-spring balance failure (12-18 years). Balance replacement costs $150-250 per window, while seal failure often requires full sash replacement ($300-500). At these failure rates, repair costs may exceed 40% of original window price within 15 years.
