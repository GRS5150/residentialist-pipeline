# Baseline Research: Ann Sacks (tile and stone)
# Compiled: 2026-09-04 (chat session; NOT scored — tile calibration exists in repo; confirm applicability)

## BRAND
Ann Sacks — founded 1981 in Portland, Oregon. **A Kohler subsidiary.** ~21 showrooms and slab galleries across North America. Range now spans tile, stone slabs, stone furniture, fireplaces, baths, accessories.
**Manufacturing:** the MADE ceramic line is handcrafted to order at the Portland factory (molded, glazed, finished by hand). Other collections sourced globally, including Moroccan zellige made by sixth- and seventh-generation artisans.
**Channel:** showroom and trade program (architects, designers, fabricators).

## MARKET PLACEMENT (Residentialist database)
- 53 mentions after cleanup, spread across the upper bands. Rare, and top-heavy.
- Cleanup note: was previously split across Plumbing and Countertops categories; consolidated to Tile. (Ann Sacks does sell plumbing and stone, so the split was not purely an error — revisit if the categories need to be separated properly later.)

## SERVICE / VALUE REALITY
- Consistent theme in consumer reviews: the design consultation and showroom experience rate well; the value proposition is contested. One direct claim that comparable quality and style is available elsewhere at a third of the price.
- Logistics friction reported: refusal of customer pickup with $300+ freight charged on two boxes; a made-to-order batch arriving with plastic separators melted onto the face of 40%+ of the tile, with no remedy from either Ann Sacks or Kohler.
- Positive counter-reports on the design service specifically (free consults, knowledgeable staff).

## TIER FLAGS FOR SCORER
- This is a specification and design brand more than a performance brand. For a tile rubric, technical properties (PEI, absorption, slip) belong to the individual collection and material, not the label.
- Handmade lines carry inherent variation — that is the point, and it is also what generates the "defect" complaints. A rubric must not treat artisan variation as a defect.
- The Kohler ownership matters for the maker pages: it is a house brand of a giant, not an independent artisan house, and both facts are true at once.

## TIER PLACEMENT IN THIS HOUSE
Ann Sacks is the conspicuous finish spend in an otherwise envelope-first allocation. That contrast is the story, and it holds whether the tile scores well or not.
