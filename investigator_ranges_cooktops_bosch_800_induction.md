### Platform Disclosure
**MANDATORY BSH PLATFORM SHARING**: Bosch 800 shares core BSH induction platform with Thermador and Gaggenau — same E.G.O. coils, same Infineon IGBTs, same Schott CERAN glass-ceramic. Critical difference: Bosch uses simpler 4-channel fixed/flex zone architecture while Thermador Freedom's full-surface 48-56 element array is unique hardware NOT shared with Bosch. This platform sharing explains why Bosch (1.7% service rate) significantly outperforms Thermador (4.9%) — same base components, but Freedom adds 4x the electronic complexity.

### Key Components
**Coil Supplier**: E.G.O.-Elektro-Gerätebau (German) — same as Thermador  
**IGBT Supplier**: Infineon Technologies — industry standard  
**Glass-Ceramic**: Schott CERAN (premium German glass-ceramic)  
**Control Failure Pattern**: 74HC4053 triple 2-channel analog multiplexer IC documented by EEVBlog  
**Architecture**: 4-channel inverter with flex-zone bridging capability

### Strengths
- **Best-in-class reliability**: 1.7% service rate beats category average by 55% and even premium Thermador by 65%
- **Superior parts ecosystem**: BSH parts universally stocked, every independent technician can service, shared platform means parts interchangeability
- **Premium glass-ceramic**: Schott CERAN provides excellent heat distribution and durability vs mid-tier alternatives

### Deficiencies
- **Limited power ceiling**: 3700W maximum falls short of premium induction standards (Thermador Freedom hits 4600W)
- **Known control board vulnerability**: EEVBlog-documented 4053 multiplexer IC failure pattern, though rare in practice given overall service rate
- **Basic interface design**: Touch slider controls less intuitive than full touchscreen systems

### What You Should Know
This is induction done right at the mid-premium level — you get genuine German components (E.G.O. coils, Schott glass) with the most reliable service record in the category. FlexInduction zones bridge to handle large cookware like griddles and stock pots. You'll need induction-compatible cookware (magnetic test: if a magnet sticks to the bottom, it works). The 1.7% service rate means 98+ units out of 100 never need repair during ownership. Power output handles most cooking tasks but won't match the raw performance of $4000+ premium units.

### Repair Economics
Most common repair: Touch control board replacement due to 4053 multiplexer IC failure (~$400-$600 installed). Power board/IGBT failures possible but rare given 1.7% service rate (~$500-$800 installed). On a $1800-$2200 cooktop, both repairs are cost-justified under the 50% rule. BSH's superior parts availability means repairs are feasible vs brands where parts scarcity forces replacement.

### Score Justification
The 70/100 reflects a well-executed mid-premium product with genuine strengths and acceptable limitations. Outstanding reliability (78 Durability) and quality components (68 Quality) offset moderate performance ceiling (64 Performance). This isn't cutting-edge technology, but it's proven induction engineering with the service record to back it up.
