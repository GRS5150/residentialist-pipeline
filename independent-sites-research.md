## CATEGORY 1: APPLIANCES (Kitchen & Laundry)

### Appliantology.org
- **URL:** appliantology.org
- **Category:** Appliances — refrigerators, washers, dryers, dishwashers, ranges, microwaves
- **Who runs it:** "Master Samurai Tech" — a working appliance repair technician and trainer; membership-based community of professional repair techs
- **Active since:** Early 2000s; actively posting February 2026
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — no manufacturer affiliation; membership fees fund the site
- **Depth level:** Professional opinion / hands-on repair data — techs share actual failure modes, circuit diagrams, and real-world reliability patterns from their bench experience
- **What makes it valuable:** This is where working appliance technicians go to solve problems and share what they see in the field. The failure patterns that emerge from thousands of tech-to-tech repair discussions are the closest thing to real-world reliability data available outside manufacturer warranty departments. Techs openly discuss which brands they see fail repeatedly and why.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability (primary)
- **Caveats:** Not a formal testing program — data is experiential and anecdotal rather than controlled. Requires parsing forum threads rather than reading structured reports. Some content paywalled for non-members.

### Yale Appliance Blog
- **URL:** yaleappliance.com/blog
- **Category:** Appliances
- **Who runs it:** Yale Appliance (Boston-area retailer) — specifically their in-house service department
- **Active since:** ~2010
- **Independence level:** Has conflicts — they sell the products they review; however their service call rate data comes from actual repair tickets, which is harder to fabricate
- **Depth level:** Data aggregation — service call rates by brand pulled from their own repair records
- **What makes it valuable:** The service call rate data is the closest thing to a consumer-accessible appliance reliability database. Published annually. Covers major brands with real percentages.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability
- **Caveats:** Significant conflict of interest — they don't carry every brand, skewing coverage. Treat as one data point, not a primary source.

**GAP NOTE:** No StarCraft-equivalent for appliances — no non-profit that independently purchases, teardowns, and publishes appliance analysis at that level. Appliantology is the closest available source. The field relies on service technician experience rather than controlled lab testing.

## CATEGORY 2: HVAC SYSTEMS

### HVAC-Talk.com
- **URL:** hvac-talk.com
- **Category:** HVAC — furnaces, ACs, heat pumps, mini-splits, air handlers, thermostats
- **Who runs it:** Community-run forum; primarily HVAC contractors and technicians
- **Active since:** Late 1990s / early 2000s; confirmed active
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — no manufacturer control; professionals speak candidly
- **Depth level:** Professional opinion — working techs and contractors discuss brand reliability, failure patterns, parts availability, and technical support quality from real installation and service experience
- **What makes it valuable:** Deep archives of candid contractor conversations about which brands fail, which have good support, and which come back for service most often. The "Most and Least Reliable Brands for AC" thread type has hundreds of professional responses spanning years. Manufacturers cannot buy this.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability (primary), Durability
- **Caveats:** Forum-format data requires active reading and synthesis. Contractor preferences can reflect regional distributor relationships. Not controlled testing.

### BuildingScience.com
- **URL:** buildingscience.com
- **Category:** HVAC (building envelope interaction, moisture, air sealing), Windows, Insulation
- **Who runs it:** Dr. Joseph Lstiburek, P.Eng. — leading North American building scientist; operates through Building Science Corporation
- **Active since:** Late 1990s; 20+ year archive
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — BSC does some government and industry consulting; published articles are science-driven and largely independent
- **Depth level:** Hands-on testing / first-principles analysis — Lstiburek conducts controlled testing and forensic investigation of building failures
- **What makes it valuable:** The most authoritative independent voice on how HVAC systems interact with building envelopes — moisture, air sealing, ventilation strategy. Routinely publishes findings manufacturers would prefer to suppress. Referenced constantly by architects, engineers, and code officials.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability, Durability, Material Safety (IAQ/moisture/mold)
- **Caveats:** Not a product review site in the traditional sense — focused on system performance and building science principles rather than brand-specific ratings. Consulting relationships create some potential conflicts.

## CATEGORY 3: WINDOWS AND DOORS

### GreenBuildingAdvisor.com
- **URL:** greenbuildingadvisor.com
- **Category:** Windows, Doors, Building Envelope
- **Who runs it:** Fine Homebuilding / Taunton Press; Q&A community of building professionals, architects, and serious homeowners
- **Active since:** ~2009; deep archive
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — affiliated with Fine Homebuilding (respected trade publication); Q&A community has no manufacturer control
- **Depth level:** Professional opinion / mixed — experts including Lstiburek, Martin Holladay, and other building science authorities answer specific window brand questions with real technical depth
- **What makes it valuable:** The Q&A archive contains the most candid professional discussions of specific window brands (Fibertec, Thermotech, Alpen, Marvin, Andersen) by builders and architects who have actually installed them. Frank failure mode discussions and assessments of manufacturer claims.
- **Residentialist axis:** Durability (primary), Reliability
- **Caveats:** Content quality varies by contributor. Not a controlled testing program. Some sections require subscription.

### NFRC.org (National Fenestration Rating Council)
- **URL:** nfrc.org
- **Category:** Windows and Doors (energy performance ratings)
- **Who runs it:** Non-profit standards body — industry, government, and consumer representatives
- **Active since:** 1989
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — testing methodology is independent; manufacturers pay for testing and participate in governance
- **Depth level:** Data aggregation — the definitive database of certified window U-factors, SHGC, VT, and air leakage ratings
- **What makes it valuable:** Every window label in the US comes from NFRC-certified testing done in independent labs. The authoritative source for comparing thermal performance across brands.
- **Residentialist axis:** Durability (energy performance), Reliability
- **Caveats:** Covers energy performance only — not structural durability, seal longevity, or hardware reliability. Manufacturers select which products to certify.

### AAMA / FGIA Certification (fenestrationmasters.com)
- **URL:** fenestrationmasters.com (FGIA) / aamanet.org (legacy)
- **Category:** Windows and Doors (structural, air, water performance)
- **Who runs it:** Fenestration & Glazing Industry Alliance (FGIA), formerly AAMA — industry standards body
- **Active since:** 1936 (AAMA); merged into FGIA 2020
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — industry-governed; testing done in independent labs; Gold label certification requires highest performance grade with independent verification
- **Depth level:** Data aggregation — certified product database covering air infiltration, water resistance, and structural performance ratings
- **What makes it valuable:** Complements NFRC by covering structural and weather performance rather than energy performance. AAMA Gold label means independently verified structural, air, and water performance. Useful for luxury home spec review where extreme weather performance matters.
- **Residentialist axis:** Durability, Reliability
- **Caveats:** Industry-governed. Only covers what manufacturers choose to certify.

### Phius Window & Door Certification (phius.org)
- **URL:** phius.org/certifications/products/phius-window-door-certification-program
- **Category:** Windows and Doors (passive house level performance)
- **Who runs it:** Phius (Passive House Institute US) — non-profit standards organization
- **Active since:** ~2012
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — third-party verified performance data
- **Depth level:** Hands-on testing / data — independently verified thermal performance exceeding standard NFRC testing; searchable certified product database
- **What makes it valuable:** For luxury custom homes in climate-sensitive markets (Austin heat, Hill Country temperature swings), Phius-certified windows represent the highest independently verified thermal performance tier. The database shows which manufacturers have submitted to the most rigorous independent testing.
- **Residentialist axis:** Durability, Reliability
- **Caveats:** Only relevant for high-performance new construction; coverage limited to manufacturers who pursue certification.

**GAP NOTE:** No independent testing site specifically tracks window seal failure rates, hardware longevity, or frame material durability over time. The most consequential durability questions about windows — when do seals fail? which frames warp? which hardware corrodes? — have no independent consumer-accessible data source.

## CATEGORY 4: FLOORING

### FloorDetective.com
- **URL:** floordetective.com
- **Category:** Flooring — hardwood, laminate, LVT/LVP, carpet, tile
- **Who runs it:** Network of certified independent floor inspectors; NWFA-certified
- **Active since:** ~2010; confirmed active through 2025
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — inspector network is independent; they are hired by homeowners, attorneys, and insurance companies to investigate claims against manufacturers and installers
- **Depth level:** Hands-on testing / data aggregation — maintains a "Defect Claims Library" documenting common manufacturing defects across all flooring categories with photos and technical analysis
- **What makes it valuable:** The Defect Claims Library is the closest thing to an independent flooring failure database that exists. Because inspectors are hired to adjudicate warranty disputes, they accumulate documented evidence of manufacturing defects that manufacturers would prefer to settle quietly. Pattern analysis across their cases would reveal which products fail and how.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability, Durability, Material Safety
- **Caveats:** Defect Claims Library is paywalled for professional subscribers. Not a product review site — it's a claims documentation tool. Requires relationship with an inspector or subscription to access depth.

### SCS Global / FloorScore (scsglobalservices.com)
- **URL:** scsglobalservices.com/services/floorscore
- **Category:** Flooring — vinyl, laminate, wood, tile (VOC emissions)
- **Who runs it:** SCS Global Services — independent certification body
- **Active since:** 2003
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — third-party testing; manufacturers pay for certification, which creates some conflict
- **Depth level:** Data aggregation — tests for 35 individual VOC emissions including formaldehyde; maintains certified product database
- **What makes it valuable:** FloorScore is more rigorous than GreenGuard for flooring specifically, testing 35 VOCs vs. GreenGuard's broader approach. Certification requires ongoing surveillance testing, not just a one-time pass. Searchable certified product database allows verification of specific products.
- **Residentialist axis:** Material Safety (primary)
- **Caveats:** Certification = passing threshold; exact emission levels not always published publicly. Voluntary program — some products comply but don't pursue certification.

### Berkeley Analytical (berkeleyanalytical.com)
- **URL:** berkeleyanalytical.com
- **Category:** Flooring (formaldehyde testing), also paints and composite wood products
- **Who runs it:** Independent accredited laboratory; UC Berkeley spinout
- **Active since:** ~1990s; active
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — fee-for-service lab, no manufacturer affiliation
- **Depth level:** Hands-on testing — actually measures formaldehyde emissions from field-collected flooring samples using standardized ASTM methods
- **What makes it valuable:** Publishes technical white papers on formaldehyde testing methodology for flooring products. Has published studies on field-collected samples showing real-world emissions vs. claimed compliance. One of the few labs willing to publish findings that contradict manufacturer claims.
- **Residentialist axis:** Material Safety (primary)
- **Caveats:** Not a consumer-facing review site — a lab that publishes technical papers. Useful as a primary data source and methodology reference, not as a product comparison tool.

**GAP NOTE:** No StarCraft-equivalent for flooring product quality, durability, or wear layer performance. The category has regulatory-adjacent sources for VOC/formaldehyde (FloorScore, CARB, Berkeley Analytical) but nothing independently testing wear layer longevity, core quality, dimensional stability, or LVP composition. This remains a full gap for product quality review.

## CATEGORY 5: PLUMBING FIXTURES (Beyond Faucets)

### TerryLove.com
- **URL:** terrylove.com/forums
- **Category:** Plumbing — all fixtures, pipes, water heaters, toilets, valves, showers
- **Who runs it:** Founded by Terry Love, master plumber (Seattle area). **Terry Love passed away in January 2020** (cancer). Forum maintained by community.
- **Active since:** Late 1990s; still active post-founder death
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — no manufacturer control; professionals speak candidly
- **Depth level:** Professional opinion / hands-on experience — working plumbers discuss fixture quality, cartridge reliability, valve failures, and installation issues from real-world experience
- **What makes it valuable:** Two decades of archived professional plumber conversations about which fixtures hold up and which fail. Depth on toilets, shower valves, and water heaters is unmatched outside manufacturer service departments. StarCraft covers faucets — TerryLove is the equivalent for the rest of plumbing.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability, Durability
- **Caveats:** Founder passed in 2020 — no active steward. Monitor for decline. Forum-format data is not structured. One RipoffReport complaint alleges bias in some areas. Verify current activity before relying on heavily.

### MaP Testing (map-testing.com)
- **URL:** map-testing.com
- **Category:** Toilets specifically
- **Who runs it:** Independent testing program; originally sponsored by water utilities and municipal conservation agencies
- **Active since:** Early 2000s; confirmed active
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — explicitly states "not affiliated with nor controlled by any manufacturer or group"
- **Depth level:** Hands-on testing — physically flushes measured quantities of soybean paste and toilet paper through every tested toilet model and measures complete waste removal. Controlled, repeatable, quantitative testing.
- **What makes it valuable:** The only place to find actual, independent toilet flush performance data that isn't self-reported by manufacturers. MaP scores show how many grams of waste a toilet clears on a single flush — results vary by 10x between models. Manufacturers with poor scores cannot suppress the data.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability, Durability
- **Caveats:** Covers flush performance only — not trap-way longevity, flapper reliability, or tank component durability. Participation is voluntary — some manufacturers may not submit all products.

## CATEGORY 6: LIGHTING FIXTURES

### DesignLights Consortium (designlights.org)
- **URL:** designlights.org
- **Category:** LED lighting — commercial and residential fixtures, lamps, controls
- **Who runs it:** Non-profit organization; funded by member utilities and energy efficiency programs
- **Active since:** 2001
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — utility-funded, not manufacturer-funded; manufacturers pay application fees
- **Depth level:** Data aggregation — Qualified Products List (QPL) of LED products that have passed independent laboratory testing for performance, efficacy, and reliability
- **What makes it valuable:** The QPL is the closest thing to a curated list of LED products independently verified against label claims. Products tested by independent labs. Particularly useful for fixture-level performance rather than just bulbs.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability, Durability
- **Caveats:** Primarily commercial-focused; residential product coverage improving but not comprehensive. Tests performance at a point in time — does not track long-term driver failure rates.

### DOE Lighting Facts Archive (energy.gov/eere/ssl/led-lighting-facts)
- **URL:** energy.gov/eere/ssl/led-lighting-facts
- **Category:** LED lighting
- **Who runs it:** U.S. Department of Energy (PNNL as technical partner)
- **Active since:** ~2009; program concluded ~2018; archive remains
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — government-purchased products tested in independent labs
- **Depth level:** Hands-on testing — DOE purchased products off-shelf and caught manufacturers with inflated claims
- **What makes it valuable:** Historical data revealed significant gaps between manufacturer claims and actual measured performance. The PNNL technical reports (pnnl.gov/advanced-lighting) documenting LED flicker, lumen maintenance, and driver quality testing are the most rigorous publicly available independent LED analysis.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability (efficacy claims), Durability (lumen maintenance)
- **Caveats:** Program concluded — not a living database. Technology has changed significantly since 2018.

**GAP NOTE:** No active, comprehensive independent review site exists for residential LED fixture quality covering driver reliability, thermal management, and long-term lumen maintenance in decorative fixtures. CandlePower Forums (candlepowerforums.com) has technically deep LED teardowns but is focused exclusively on portable lighting (flashlights), not residential fixtures. This is a genuine gap.

## CATEGORY 7: COUNTERTOPS AND SURFACES

### NIOSH / CDC — Engineered Stone Silicosis Data (cdc.gov/niosh)
- **URL:** cdc.gov/niosh
- **Category:** Countertops — specifically engineered stone / quartz
- **Who runs it:** National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (federal government)
- **Active since:** Ongoing surveillance; major publications 2019, 2023, 2024, January 2026
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — federal health agency
- **Depth level:** Hands-on data — surveillance of actual silicosis cases among fabrication workers; material composition testing
- **What makes it valuable:** The primary authoritative source on the silicosis risk associated with engineered stone (quartz) countertops. Key facts: quartz countertops contain 90%+ crystalline silica. By late 2024, 219 confirmed US worker silicosis cases including 14 deaths. Published January 2026. This is an active and worsening public health situation that homeowners are largely unaware of. The risk is to fabricators, not homeowners — but homeowners are making material selections with no knowledge of the downstream occupational health implications.
- **Residentialist axis:** Material Safety (primary)
- **Caveats:** Covers occupational health of fabricators, not consumer exposure. Does not rate brands — the crystalline silica content is inherent to engineered stone regardless of brand.

### MyChemicalFreeHouse.net
- **URL:** mychemicalfreehouse.net
- **Category:** Cross-category — covers flooring, paint, countertops, cabinets, adhesives from a material safety / IAQ perspective
- **Who runs it:** Individual blogger; focused on creating chemical-safe homes for environmentally sensitive occupants
- **Active since:** ~2019; updated September 2025
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — no manufacturer affiliation or advertising revenue evident
- **Depth level:** Data aggregation / professional opinion — synthesizes certification data, manufacturer disclosures, and health research; notably critical of green certifications
- **What makes it valuable:** One of the few consumer-facing sources that critically analyzes the limitations of GreenGuard and FloorScore certifications — pointing out that certified products don't disclose actual emission levels, only that they passed a threshold. Covers VOC implications of quartz countertops, specific flooring materials, and paints from a first-principles safety perspective.
- **Residentialist axis:** Material Safety (primary)
- **Caveats:** Individual blogger — not a testing organization. Methodology is research synthesis, not primary testing. Some content oriented toward extreme chemical sensitivity rather than average homeowner thresholds.

**GAP NOTE:** No independent consumer-facing testing site covers countertop durability, stain resistance, heat resistance, or impact resistance by brand. The quartz resin content issue (lower-grade quartz uses 10-30% resin vs. premium's 6-8%, dramatically affecting hardness and stain resistance) is documented in industry discussions but no one has independently verified which specific brands use what formulations. Residentialist has genuine first-mover opportunity here — particularly on the engineered stone silicosis angle, which is newsworthy and almost completely unknown among luxury homeowners.

## CATEGORY 8: ROOFING

### IBHS.org (Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety)
- **URL:** ibhs.org
- **Category:** Roofing — asphalt shingles, metal, tile; also windows, doors, and whole-structure resilience
- **Who runs it:** Non-profit research organization; funded by the insurance industry (property-casualty insurers)
- **Active since:** 1994; major test facility operational since ~2010
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — insurance-funded (not manufacturer-funded); insurers have direct financial interest in accurate failure data
- **Depth level:** Hands-on testing / teardown-level — full-scale test facility in South Carolina capable of simulating hurricane-force winds, hail, driven rain, and wildfire on actual structures. Has publicly named products that failed testing.
- **What makes it valuable:** The only organization in the US regularly conducting full-scale physical destruction tests on roofing and building products. Their FORTIFIED standard is based on this testing. Deep reports on shingle granule loss, wind uplift, hail impact, and metal roof performance. Manufacturers cannot suppress results.
- **Residentialist axis:** Durability (primary), Reliability
- **Caveats:** Insurance-industry funded creates bias toward identifying failure (relevant to insurance payouts). Focus is heavily on catastrophic event performance rather than gradual weathering and aging. Not all roofing products are tested.

### RoofingTalk.com
- **URL:** roofingtalk.com
- **Category:** Roofing — all types
- **Who runs it:** Community forum; professional roofing contractors (sister site to ContractorTalk.com)
- **Active since:** Mid-2000s; confirmed active
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — professional contractor forum
- **Depth level:** Professional opinion — working roofers discuss brand preferences, installation experiences, and field failure patterns
- **What makes it valuable:** Candid contractor opinion on shingle brands (GAF vs. CertainTeed vs. Owens Corning vs. TAMKO), metal roofing systems, flat roof membranes, and underlayment quality. Professional consensus across thread archives is a reliable signal on which products contractors trust.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability, Durability
- **Caveats:** Contractor preferences can be influenced by regional distributor relationships and manufacturer training/credentialing programs (GAF in particular has aggressive contractor incentive programs that may color opinions).

## CATEGORY 9: PAINT AND COATINGS

### PaintTalk.com
- **URL:** painttalk.com
- **Category:** Paint — interior, exterior, industrial coatings
- **Who runs it:** Community forum; professional painting contractors
- **Active since:** Mid-2000s; confirmed active
- **Independence level:** Fully independent — professional contractor forum with no manufacturer control
- **Depth level:** Professional opinion — working painters discuss product quality, application characteristics, durability, and what they recommend and avoid
- **What makes it valuable:** Professional painters have the most honest opinions about paint quality because their reputation depends on results. PaintTalk contains frank assessments of Sherwin-Williams, Benjamin Moore, and other brands — including documented quality decline over time (multiple threads noting SW quality dropped significantly in recent years). Contractors discuss real-world performance: coverage, washability, long-term adhesion, and blocking ability.
- **Residentialist axis:** Reliability, Durability
- **Caveats:** No controlled testing — all experiential. Contractor preferences can reflect pricing and availability rather than pure quality. Not organized for easy research synthesis.

### UL GREENGUARD Certification (ul.com/services/ul-greenguard-certification)
- **URL:** ul.com/services/ul-greenguard-certification
- **Category:** Paint, flooring, adhesives, sealants, countertops — VOC emissions
- **Who runs it:** UL Solutions — independent safety certification organization
- **Active since:** 2001 (Greenguard); GREENGUARD Gold added ~2011
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — third-party certification; manufacturers pay for testing, creating some conflict
- **Depth level:** Data aggregation — tests VOC emissions against established thresholds; GREENGUARD Gold has stricter limits for children's environments
- **What makes it valuable:** The most recognized independent VOC certification for paints and interior finishes. GREENGUARD Gold indicates passing a stricter threshold suitable for schools and healthcare facilities. Useful as a baseline threshold check for paint and surface product safety.
- **Residentialist axis:** Material Safety
- **Caveats:** Certified products don't disclose actual measured emission levels — only that they passed the threshold. GREENGUARD Gold is stricter than base GREENGUARD but still does not confirm "zero VOC." Limited utility for differentiating between certified products.

**GAP NOTE:** No independent paint testing lab publishes consumer-accessible durability, washability, or VOC data. The field relies on contractor experience (PaintTalk) and certification thresholds (GREENGUARD) rather than controlled comparison testing.

## CATEGORY 10: CABINETRY AND MILLWORK

### AWI (Architectural Woodwork Institute) — awinet.org
- **URL:** awinet.org / awiqcp.org
- **Category:** Cabinetry and millwork — kitchen cabinets, built-ins, doors, paneling
- **Who runs it:** Architectural Woodwork Institute — non-profit trade and standards organization
- **Active since:** 1953
- **Independence level:** Mostly independent — industry trade organization; defines standards but does not exclusively represent manufacturers
- **Depth level:** Standards / data — publishes the only widely-used independent quality grade definitions for architectural woodwork: Economy, Custom, and Premium grades with specific requirements for materials, joinery, finish, and tolerances
- **What makes it valuable:** AWI's Quality Certification Program (QCP) is the only third-party verification system that certifies whether a specific cabinet or millwork project meets defined quality standards. AWI Premium grade specifies plywood box construction, dovetail or equivalent drawer joinery, and specific finish hardness — the benchmarks that distinguish real quality from marketing language. When a cabinet manufacturer claims "Premium" without AWI certification, they're using the term freely.
- **Residentialist axis:** Durability (primary), Reliability
- **Caveats:** AWI certifies projects, not product lines — so it verifies a specific completed installation rather than a manufacturer's entire output. Industry trade group, so not fully independent. Consumer awareness of AWI standards is very low.

**GAP NOTE — FULL:** No forum, testing organization, or review site independently analyzes kitchen cabinet construction quality at the component level (box material thickness, dado vs. staple assembly, drawer slide load ratings, hinge cycle testing, finish hardness, or wood species accuracy). Houzz kitchen forum (houzz.com/discussions) has consumer discussions about cabinet brands but these are homeowner opinions, not professional analysis. No equivalent to PaintTalk or HVAC-Talk exists for cabinetry. AWI provides standards but not product testing. This is the largest and most complete gap in the entire landscape.

### Top 7 Most Valuable Sources Found

1. **map-testing.com** — The only truly StarCraft-equivalent source found: independent, quantitative, controlled physical testing, covers an entire product category (toilets). Non-profit, manufacturer-independent, cannot be suppressed.

2. **IBHS.org** — Full-scale physical destruction testing of roofing and building products. Insurance-funded independence. Unique in the industry. Deep technical reports.

3. **BuildingScience.com** — The authoritative independent voice on HVAC and building envelope interaction. 20+ year archive of rigorous science-based analysis by the leading building scientist in North America.

4. **HVAC-Talk.com** — The deepest concentration of candid professional contractor data on HVAC brand reliability. Irreplaceable for ground-truth reliability signals.

5. **Appliantology.org** — Tech-to-tech appliance repair forum. Real failure pattern data from bench experience. Active February 2026.

6. **NIOSH/CDC silicosis data** — The most consequential Residentialist intelligence in the entire landscape: 90%+ of luxury homeowners specifying quartz countertops have no idea that engineered stone fabricators are dying of silicosis at documented rates. This is an active public health crisis (219 confirmed US cases including 14 deaths as of late 2024, January 2026 NIOSH update). Homeowners are not at risk from the finished product, but they are making material selections with zero knowledge of this issue — and many spec "quartz everywhere" in luxury builds.

7. **FloorDetective.com** — Independent inspector network with a Defect Claims Library documenting manufacturing defects across all flooring categories. The only flooring failure documentation database that exists outside manufacturer warranty files.

---

### Biggest Opportunities for Residentialist

**Cabinetry** — No independent analysis exists at any level. First-principles build-quality research (box material, joinery type, drawer slide specs, finish hardness) would face zero competition.

**Countertops** — Specifically the quartz/engineered stone silicosis story and resin content quality differentiation. No one has published this for homeowners in accessible form. The NIOSH data is there but buried in public health literature.

**Flooring** — LVP/LVT quality differentiation (wear layer actual vs. claimed thickness, SPC vs. WPC core composition, formaldehyde from Chinese-sourced core materials) has no independent consumer-facing source. The market is flooded with misleading specs.

**Lighting** — Residential LED fixture driver reliability and long-term lumen maintenance has no independent consumer-facing source. The DOE program ended in 2018. DesignLights Consortium is commercial-focused.

---

### Key Intelligence Notes for Residentialist Service Design

The **silicosis angle on quartz countertops** is the single most actionable finding in this research. It is:
- Factually documented by federal health agencies
- Completely unknown to the luxury homeowner market
- Relevant to every client specifying quartz in a $500K+ custom home
- Not covered by any existing consumer product intelligence service
- An active and worsening situation (case count growing)
- Not a risk to the homeowner from the finished product — but an ethical and reputational consideration worth disclosing

This alone could be a standalone Residentialist report section for every engagement involving quartz.
